### 4. Bimanual skill transfer for changing workpieces

AIRoA released model weights and code on 6 November after learning manipulation from 16,151 hours of handheld demonstrations. Its authors report 78% average success on their ten tasks against 27% for their strongest evaluated alternative, and operation on four supported robot families. Handheld transfer already existed in UMI. A future manufacturing pilot would compare a new skill's first and repeat changeover against best manual or fixed automation, with real workpieces, authorized equipment, an operator, qualification and recovery accounted for. Poor repeat performance or cheaper conventional fixtures would falsify the business thesis. Physical operation was outside the authorized resources. ### 5. Proof of contracted model execution

Anthropic released FlashProve on 2 November. Its one large quantized-model H100 experiment reported about 110 proven tokens/s in aggregate, roughly 19 MB per 1,024-token proof and 70–95 times the native GPU cost. A procurement evaluator could require independently checked execution proof on a small subset of valuable jobs where it lacks access to the committed model. Ordinary replay, attestations and existing proof systems are strong alternatives with different trust assumptions. A pilot requires an authentic provider witness, explicit audit scope and observed costs. The thesis fails if the verifier accepts a cheaper complete alternative or the provider cannot supply timely evidence. This search did not operate a prover.
