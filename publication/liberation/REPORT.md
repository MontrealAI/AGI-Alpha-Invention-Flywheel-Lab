## Follow-up

On the already revealed later cohort, the five largest errors account for about **90% of the weighted fit’s total absolute timing error**. Median absolute errors among the three center forecasts range from 67.3 to 68.7 seconds. This diagnostic motivates checking whether apparent catastrophic errors originate in epoch association, source outliers, or real timing variation. A new comparison should freeze dated, independently extracted later transit times and adjudicate epoch associations before scoring frozen forecasts. The raw model proposals, arithmetic corrections and adjudicated next experiment are recorded. [Research continuation](NEXT.json)

I exercised the proposed cross-check with the separately reduced Ivshina and Winn timing catalogue. Exact planet-name matching yielded no overlap because the catalogue uses host-style names. A separate exploratory check required one eligible planet under the early catalogue host label and agreement with the independent published period. This matched **866 early TESS events across 39 of the 106 targets**, with two disagreements beyond both the one-minute and the three-quadrature-uncertainty flags. **None of the five largest-error targets was matched.** The two reductions share observations, so the counts are descriptive and do not measure independent observing coverage. The most direct next test is targeted re-extraction from dated multi-planet TESS light curves for the missing five, followed by a separate forecast confirmation.
