## Game and certificate

The row player publishes its policy and privately samples one observation instrument. The opponent chooses the hidden scenario and its own feasible integer allocation after seeing the policy and before seeing the draw or signal. The chosen instrument generates a signal from its declared scenario-dependent channel. The row player allocates its integer budget after observing that signal. The common payoff units cover the signed sum of scenario-specific lane weights and the fixed charge for the instrument. Each channel probability and charge is represented exactly as a rational number. The instance may include a free constant-signal option as an outside choice.

The sparse program gives each instrument a selection mass and one path flow per signal. Each such flow has the selection mass at its source. Lane cumulative probabilities produce the expected payoff against any opposing lane amount. Rational channel coefficients determine each scenario's weighted sum. Shortest-path potential inequalities then give the opponent's minimum across full allocations. The optimization maximizes the common scenario floor after the weighted acquisition charges.

Rounded integer probabilities remain feasible. An exact verifier recomputes the worst column response to the published policy and the best full instrument-and-contingent-action response to the published opponent distribution. For a fixed instrument, each signal has its own maximizing allocation. These two separately executable deviations enclose the game value, even when rounding widens the interval. A result earns `QUALIFIED` when the width is at most one part per million of the largest scenario weight total, with a scale floor of one. `BOUND_ONLY` preserves observed valid wider bounds. The verifier uses Python's standard library and the preceding release's pinned verification code. [Solver](solve_observation.py) · [Verifier](observation.py) · [Frozen plan](QUALIFICATION-PLAN.json)

If the opponent can see the selected instrument before placing its allocation, it can respond to each announced choice. The appropriate value is then the best of the separately solved instrument games. The private model depends on this timing, the supplied channel calibration and sufficient agreement on payoff units. Results concern expected worst-case payoffs for this finite game.

## Executed outcomes

The example has two hidden scenarios and one allocation credit. Each scenario pays 12 for a different lane. For either noisy instrument, a trigger is certain for its favored scenario and has probability one half for the other. Acquisition costs 2. With one announced instrument, an optimally randomized response to its trigger attains a net guarantee of 6. The instrument mixture samples each option with probability one half and gets a matched lane with probability three quarters in both scenarios, giving `12 × 3/4 − 2 = 7`. The full matrix, double oracle and sparse solve agree exactly. In this example a per-observation charge of 3 puts the mixture at the free no-information boundary of 6.
