# Selection lesson

**None of the screened opportunities is admitted as the best Super Alpha.**

- Passive joint-friction construction needs demonstrated transferable calibration. [Fu et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.19555v1) do not establish our proposed mixed-device outcome.
- Original-specification planning already exists in [Operational075](https://github.com/MontrealAI/AGI-Alpha-Invention-Flywheel-Lab/pull/164); repeating it does not establish a new activity.
- Rack-aware coding was implemented and rejected as a flagship. [Google LRC](https://www.usenix.org/system/files/fast23-kadekodi.pdf) and [UniLRC](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.06819) are serious prior art. Ordinary controls meet the service; this candidate offers tradeoffs, not enormous advantage.

A more consequential research question is portable process invention: reproducing a qualified manufacturing output on unfamiliar equipment. However, [RoboChem-Flex](https://doi.org/10.1038/s44160-026-01053-0) already offers autonomous optimization and transfer learning. No new residual beyond strong system identification/experimental design, matched physical evidence, or complete business case was established here. This is **unselected and unexecuted**, not a replacement flagship.

The transferable rule is to select an important blocked outcome before implementing a convenient benchmark, and test whether an ordinary complete route already removes that block. Do not turn small performance improvements or familiar reuse into claims of new industries.

Related [PR183](https://github.com/MontrealAI/AGI-Alpha-Invention-Flywheel-Lab/pull/183) reports the same lesson for coded matrix computation: ordinary coding/reuse matches, and replication can win. Do not duplicate that line as a new flagship.
