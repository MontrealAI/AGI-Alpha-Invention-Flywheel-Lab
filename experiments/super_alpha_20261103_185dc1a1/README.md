# SUPER ALPHA — confidential production: result and lesson

**No consequential new activity or Super Alpha advantage was established.** We built and checked executable multi-provider offers and a useful disclosure safeguard. Ordinary composition matched every case, and direct self-service produced the same mathematical outputs without the assumed supplier fragmentation. This candidate is a failed flagship hypothesis; its reusable component is retained.

## Out-learned lesson

- **Tried:** combine separately offered computational capabilities into a complete voluntary outcome while keeping submitted asks confidential across repeated and successor jobs.
- **Decisive result:** four complete bundle quotes reconstruct all four exact asks in every registered world. A stable coarse tariff protects the declared finite ambiguity, but costs acceptance. A differently grouped successor can disclose the entire vector. Ordinary methods match the construction, and self-service avoids the modeled coalition entirely.
- **Why it falls short:** the demonstration supplies its own fragmentation assumption. It does not establish a real beneficiary unable to achieve the outcome with existing means, indispensable cooperation, observed demand, or superior complete economics.
- **What must change:** independently owned complementary inputs must be actually necessary, the useful outcome and confidentiality threat model must be real, and a measured complete alternative must leave a credible consequential gap.
- **Decision:** stop promoting or scaling this matrix-product market. Retain the disclosure policy and counterexample; redirect discovery to an evidenced blocked activity. No conclusion that the frontier is closed follows.

## What was constructed and tested

[mechanism.py](mechanism.py) generates executable assignments from public typed operators, capacities, exclusions and resource limits, then actually computes an exact integer matrix-vector result. There are 24 feasible primary programs, 24 fresh successor programs and 12 shared-resource programs. This is a developer-written compiler generating executable assignments, not discovered new mathematics.

[independent.py](independent.py) independently enumerates the strongest finite complete-offer alternative, checks whole-buyer and provider participation, and computes the answer without candidate code. Ordinary public-graph and result reuse are enabled. The outer constructor and attacker do not receive the actual hidden asks. The attacker predicts public quotes using a dummy buyer, not private buyer preferences.

**The service models ideal secure internals; cryptography is not implemented.** The protected quantity is a submitted ask in a known finite domain, not true production cost, public capability descriptions, or arbitrary private knowledge. Truthful bids, strategic equilibrium, real security, real supplier consent and actual demand remain unestablished. No attack on MapXchange or another deployed product was conducted.

Four providers each have one-block capacity. The predecessor needs three blocks; a fresh successor needs four. Each private ask is one of 200, 300, 600 or 700 cents, giving 256 worlds. All 16 exclusion masks and six price caps are evaluated. The complete campaign checks **172,032 counterfactual offers, with zero ordinary-control mismatches**. These are exhaustive authored cases, not customers or conversion probabilities. All 25 focused regression tests pass locally; hosted qualification is recorded separately in [VALIDATION.json](evidence/VALIDATION.json).

| Released outputs | Minimum possible ask vectors | Exact full-vector reconstruction |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Exact predecessor quotes | 1 | 256 / 256 |
| Stable coarse predecessor and successor | 16 | 0 / 256 |
| Differently grouped successor alone | 16 | 0 / 256 |
| Predecessor plus differently grouped successor | 1 | 32 / 256 |

The four exact complete offers reveal the sums excluding each provider. Their sum divided by three is the total of all asks; subtracting a particular quote reveals the excluded ask. No singleton offer, purchase, private buyer preference or internal secret read is required.

The stable tariff maps 200/300 to 300 and 600/700 to 700. Every output factors through that same tariff vector; two raw possibilities per provider leave 16 indistinguishable worlds across repeated and compatible successor use. This is finite ambiguity, not differential privacy or protection against arbitrary side information. Repartitioning the successor changes what can be inferred jointly. The continuing implementation rejects that reset; ordinary reuse of the same rule provides identical protection.

Protection has an economic cost: accepted quote counts fall from 3,492 to 3,312 in the predecessor, 458 to 336 in the successor, and 2,088 to 1,984 in the shared-resource case. At the actual authored US$30 buyer limit with no exclusions, one successor world changes from **US$29.40 accepted to US$33.40 refused**. This preserves the adverse region without interpreting the enumeration as a market estimate.

A supplementary post-decisive [self-service control](evidence/SELF_SERVICE_CONTROL.json) produces all three mathematical outputs using ordinary direct computation. All inputs and the operation are public, so the artificial one-block supplier quota is not a demonstrated real-world barrier. No human, human-plus-AI, frontier-AI or deployed-product end-to-end superiority is claimed; those commercial comparators were not measured.
