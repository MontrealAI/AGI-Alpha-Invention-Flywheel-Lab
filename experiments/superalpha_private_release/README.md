# Private rare-case discovery: stop the strict offer

**Changed capability:** we can generate and independently check a complete release channel for the proposed synthetic contract. The experiment **does not open a new clinical activity or establish Super Alpha**. It prevents an unsupported promise from entering a commercial simulation.

Three different possibilities were considered: self-building laboratory cooperatives, private rare-case discovery, and legacy-equipment software reconstruction. Their beneficiary, arrangement, strongest inspected alternative, remaining gap and falsifier are in [the preregistration](PROTOCOL.md), published at `6cb78b9648f653c0e5f2cea21445f2e0a1a2a548` before evaluation. Lab transfer and whole-system software replacement remain unresolved, not disproved.

**Tried → result → why → next.** We constructed a linear-program channel generator with explicit refusal, a separate probability checker, and a rational upper-bound/ordinary randomized-response comparator. Ten registered cases (two/four answers × five privacy bounds) tied the strongest ordinary channel within 1e-8. Stronger privacy prevents accurate disclosure about one family's decisive contribution; encryption cannot change what an authorized plaintext answer reveals. Stop this strict offer. Retain the ordinary method; no larger sweep is justified.

For `m` mutually adjacent states with distinct correct answers, the frontier is `lambda/(lambda+m-1)`. Proof: if each state's correct answer has probability at least `t`, privacy requires at least `t/lambda` for each other answer; row normalization gives the bound. Ordinary generalized randomized response attains it. Refusal cannot improve this unconditional accuracy. Exact accuracy is impossible at any finite ratio in this subproblem. These are mathematical conditional results, **not patient data, clinical accuracy or acceptable privacy thresholds**. Different adjacency, utility, consent or larger cohorts may change applicability. Approximate DP and repeated-query operation were not tested.

The two-finding successor scores 40% jointly versus approximately 34.3% with independent releases splitting the same privacy budget. The entire gain is explained by ordinary joint design. Direct ordinary construction without the predecessor and equal-content ordinary reuse reach the same 40%. No earned capital, superior discovery method or commercial predecessor-to-successor expansion was demonstrated.

PRISM's [paper, sections 5.1–5.4](https://doi.org/10.1093/bioinformatics/btaf468), describes a trusted result recipient, committee authorization and consent/query governance. Our recipient-level differential-privacy requirement is a different contract; this result is **not an attack on PRISM**. Its source was inspected at `f67470aee23dbd774b7cae92943e008a13f5f9ca`; encrypted performance was not reproduced. After the strict test, inspecting that governance alternative was the next executed discovery step. A viable pivot now requires evidence of an institution's authorized output, useful acceptance conditions and a gap beyond existing governance. No outreach was conducted or authorized. Human, hybrid, specialist and other frontier-AI complete delivery costs remain unmeasured; no productivity superiority is claimed.
