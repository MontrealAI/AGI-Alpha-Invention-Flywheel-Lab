## Construction and limits

Frame 0 places the specimen on path A and a blank on B. Frame 1 exchanges them. A half-log double ratio cancels fixed unequal path gains and per-frame shared flux. A bounded change in the relative gains and bounded corrected-reading error propagate to material intervals. The retained instrument then supports a new K2 decision: which cells exceed copper density 0.10 g/cm³, with an interval for their area. The threshold and ideal geometry are authored, not an industrial safety standard.

The gained scope is relative to the inherited single-ratio acquisition. Ordinary calibration algebra and an equally informed joint fit supply strong alternatives. The result establishes no superior general invention procedure or unique enabling asset. At the declared single-specimen-exposure cap, pairing is ineligible while a blank-calibrated method uses one specimen exposure. Equal signal-error bounds do not imply equal photons, dose, elapsed time or cost.

The retained counterexamples matter. Excess coherent gain drift, a wrong coefficient basis and a nonblank reference can produce accepted wrong material values. A fourth witness gives two different spatial truths with the same observations; one call returns a confident area that is wrong in one world. These are explicit failures outside the adopted model assumptions, not successfully detected anomalies. A physical instrument must qualify the exchange geometry, spectral response, true blank, dark correction and error/drift bounds separately.
