# Opportunity selection: paired acquisition

Date: 2 November 2026. This is a prospective selection record, not an evaluation result. Primary sources, exact editions and access limits are recorded in [SOURCES.md](SOURCES.md). Numerical acceptance rules belong to the experiment's frozen protocol.

## Decision and comparison

Select a bounded X-ray acquisition construction that exchanges the sample and reference between two physical paths. Its immediate dependency is the coherent gain ambiguity demonstrated by the [earlier spectral-interface experiment](https://github.com/MontrealAI/AGI-Alpha-Invention-Flywheel-Lab/blob/8c13a60859f58c5b0a332eaa433f2ff84c584e8a/experiments/public_portfolio_20261102/interface/REPORT.md). Removing that ambiguity could make a retained material-measurement interface useful over a larger, explicitly qualified class of instrument states. Whether it does so, and whether conventional calibration already provides the complete result more economically, are test questions.

The ranking considers the importance of a missing physical capability, the ability to distinguish success from failure, and the strength of the incumbent. Ready access alone does not establish a productive opportunity.

## What changes in the selected construction

The earlier interface normalized simultaneous sample and reference observations under a common-gain assumption. Its published counterexample introduced a material-like difference between paths that the spectral residual could not distinguish from real material. The successor changes acquisition, so that the same specimen and reference encounter both paths. Under the declared multiplicative model and stable gains during the pair, cancellation can remove dependence on the unknown fixed gain ratio. This is a new dependency treatment relative to that inspected experiment; substitution, reversal and ratio calibration have extensive conventional antecedents.

K1 must establish its allowed transmission/material decisions before K2 uses them in spatial reconstruction. K2 has its own geometry, uncertainty and acceptance obligations. Reusing coefficients or code is not evidence that a new scanner, spectrum, specimen or detector is qualified. The two public manuscripts inform this separation of purpose, construction and future scope; their finite theorems are not proofs of this physical apparatus.

## Productive consequence and refusal conditions

A possible beneficiary is a laboratory or inspection-system developer that needs reliable material inference despite calibration drift. A possible retained asset is a versioned acquisition procedure, implementation, applicability record and independently checked error model. These are hypotheses for later qualification. No buyer demand, accepted price, exclusive rights, installed instrument, financing or newly attainable industry is established.

A complete comparison must count added exposures, actual acquisition duration, specimen/reference handling, alignment, calibration standards, drift checks, development, failed attempts and maintenance. Radiation dose and instrument costs remain modeled unless measured. Withholding calibration data does not create physical savings. A conventional route that achieves the same accepted outcome within the same complete budget defeats a claimed enabling advantage.

The initial multiplicative cancellation does not cover arbitrary inter-exposure differential drift, additive scatter or offset, nonlinear detector response, pileup, finite spectral bandwidth, changed ray geometry or an incorrect reference. Repeated agreement of a residual cannot certify those assumptions. Such changes require a separately justified model and fresh qualification, or refusal to reuse. Non-entry and retaining the existing complete instrument remain legitimate outcomes.
