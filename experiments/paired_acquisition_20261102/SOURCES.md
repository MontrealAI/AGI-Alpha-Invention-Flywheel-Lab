## 3. Conventional calibration antecedents

- **NIST, [Spectral comparator facilities](https://www.nist.gov/laboratories/tools-instruments/spectral-comparator-facilities)**: substitution places a test detector and known standard in the same optical path; simultaneous monitor measurements compensate source fluctuations. Its double-ratio measurement equation retains the ratio of preamplifier gains. This supports a strong conventional substitution/two-beam comparator, not unrestricted immunity to unequal or changing gain.
- **Travis, Smith, Rasberry and Kramer, [NIST SP 260-140](https://nvlpubs.nist.gov/nistpubs/Legacy/SP/nistspecialpublication260-140.pdf)**, February 2000, printed pp. 25–27: an unequal-beam transmittance is divided by a no-sample baseline at the same wavelength. Baseline correction adds noise; the document specifies daily or more frequent baseline acquisition and reference readings immediately before and after a sample for single-beam operation. This is a concrete conventional alternative and acquisition burden.
- **F. J. J. Clarke, [High Accuracy Spectrophotometry at the National Physical Laboratory](https://doi.org/10.6028/jres.076A.036)**, 1972, §§III and IV; [full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6716048/): reference observations bracket sample measurements, and a kinematic carriage exchanges samples in the beam. The discussion treats detector linearity, masking, stray light and geometric consistency as separate accuracy requirements. Optical substitution is an antecedent, not a validation of the proposed X-ray fixture.
- **Brookhaven-hosted [Appendix: Polarimeter](https://www.bnl.gov/edm/review/files/pdf/estephenson_polarimeter_main_appendix.pdf)**, PDF p. 5, “Systematic Error Management”: a cross ratio combines opposite detectors and reversed polarization states to cancel acceptance and state luminosity factors. It explicitly retains sensitivity to geometry, polarization differences, gain shifts and pileup beyond its cancellation assumptions. This primary technical document is a cross-domain antecedent for multiplicative cancellation, not evidence of X-ray performance. The accessed appendix has no publication date on its title page; none is invented here.

The inference for this experiment is limited: exchanging which known/unknown transmission occupies each path can make fixed multiplicative factors cancel, provided the new acquisition actually preserves the necessary state and pairing. The authoritative mathematical model and controls belong to the frozen experiment protocol.

## 4. NIST SRD126 coefficients and rights

Primary source: J. H. Hubbell and S. M. Seltzer, [NIST Standard Reference Database 126](https://www.nist.gov/pml/x-ray-mass-attenuation-coefficients), DOI [10.18434/T4D01F](https://doi.org/10.18434/T4D01F). NIST identifies July 2004 as the last update to data content. The following values were checked directly against the element tables, not inferred from a plot.

| Nominal energy | Aluminum, Z=13 | Copper, Z=29 | Iron, Z=26 |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 50 keV = 0.050 MeV | 0.3681 | 2.613 | 1.958 |
| 100 keV = 0.100 MeV | 0.1704 | 0.4584 | 0.3717 |
| 150 keV = 0.150 MeV | 0.1378 | 0.2217 | 0.1964 |

Values are **mass attenuation coefficients, μ/ρ, in cm²/g**, from [Al](https://physics.nist.gov/PhysRefData/XrayMassCoef/ElemTab/z13.html), [Cu](https://physics.nist.gov/PhysRefData/XrayMassCoef/ElemTab/z29.html) and [Fe](https://physics.nist.gov/PhysRefData/XrayMassCoef/ElemTab/z26.html). They are not the adjacent mass energy-absorption coefficients. Multiplication by mass areal density in g/cm² gives a dimensionless Beer–Lambert exponent. Iron is the predecessor's model-mismatch challenge, not part of its fitted Al/Cu basis; its use in a successor must be explicitly registered.

These numbers are reference inputs, not photons measured by this experiment. Monochromatic attenuation does not supply a calibrated source spectrum, detector response, scatter model or coefficient-uncertainty budget.

**Rights:** SRD126 displays a Secretary of Commerce copyright notice. [NIST's licensing policy](https://www.nist.gov/open/license) distinguishes SRD from other government data generally free of US copyright. This small attributed research extract must not be relabeled CC0/public domain, an exclusive asset or a blanket commercial database license. Commercial redistribution rights remain unresolved in this experiment. The live HTML was inspected; no archival byte identity or download hash is invented.
