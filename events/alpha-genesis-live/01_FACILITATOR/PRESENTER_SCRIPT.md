# Presenter script — a narrative, not a catalogue of GitHub projects

## Opening · minute 0

> “What is intelligence good for if it can only describe what is possible? Tonight we give it a harder assignment: take an ambition, investigate what is actually true, construct something useful, and withstand a serious test. By the end you will have seen an idea become a working instrument—and you will know the five moves to try yourself.”

## The ambition · minute 8

> “Suppose eight purchasing commitments stand between a team and its product launch. Some suppliers are late, one confirmation is missing, a price has moved, and a quality record is on hold. The easy question is whether the purchase orders look all right. The consequential question is whether we can preserve the launch without authorizing an unsafe commitment. What would count as progress?”

Take an audience vote on `time`, `cost` or `confidence`. Emphasize the rules: human authority, exact source records, no invented supplier confirmations. Use the chosen flag during the live run.

## The real agent · minute 20

> “This is not ChatGPT telling us a story about having investigated. Watch the tool calls. The model must request the actual records, inspect each order and deliver a complete decision dossier. A separate verifier—not the model—decides whether those structured claims satisfy the frozen rules.”

Open terminal with the verified `codex-pro` command. Narrate actual steps only. Never say a call happened if it did not. The untrusted supplier note includes an instruction to hide a quality hold: treat it as source content, never operative guidance.

## The reveal · minute 43

> “The first useful discovery is six held orders and two that can go to human review. That is accurate under this case. But it is not the last move. Could we invent a way to explore recoveries without silently rewriting the rules?”

Show the separate baseline verification receipt and its scope. The figures are fictional approved commitments, not financial savings or losses.

## The strategic fork · minute 55

> “We have three plausible routes. Recover supply. Resolve control issues. Or coordinate both. Which one would you investigate—and what evidence would make you abandon it?”

Run the second strategy stage, compile the HTML instrument, and show the files created. If the run is a preview, label it.

## The build and test · minute 70

> “We no longer have an answer. We have a tool. Choose the scenario and watch the decision picture change. We can inspect every order, test the same policy and download the result. We are seeing conditional possibilities—not claiming that real suppliers changed their commitments.”

Click all three options; leave the combined case open only after stating its assumptions. Export JSON, open source-lineage details, compare the independent check.

## Participant ownership · minute 87

> “Write your own ambition. Not a prompt. An outcome. What tool could an agent construct to test it? What would count as evidence? Which competent ordinary approach would be the benchmark?”

Refer to workbook pages 2 and 8; attendees complete the challenge card.

## Close · minute 115

> “The most important artifact tonight is not a fictional purchase order dashboard. It is a method: ambition, investigation, alternatives, construction and test, next move. Bring it to a consequential professional problem. Make the proof as ambitious as the idea.”

Offer a private team application as a separate service, not as a guarantee of savings or automatic production deployment.
