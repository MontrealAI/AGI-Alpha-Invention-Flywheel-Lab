## Compare three materially different business arrangements

| Candidate | Buyer and completed outcome | Distribution / coordination | Decisive constraint and smallest actual experiment |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| A: low-ticket individual digital repairs | SMB merchant purchases a one-time source-bound fix | Paid per-lead acquisition | At $20 complete-cost profit/order, **50 million accepted, collected orders per year** are required for $1B. Test real comparable acceptance, costed lead acquisition and repeat need; commodity price pressure can erase margin. |
| **B: recurring, independently verifiable data/evidence operations** | B2B enterprise purchases a continuous outcome: reconciliations, exceptions, change-aware evidence and accepted decision reports | Authorized enterprise channel partner plus direct sales, recurring contract or scoped usage | Existing qualified reconciliation/checking code is a technical starting point. The missing means are genuinely new unique customer work, partner-sourced qualified demand, permissioned access and complete-contract acceptance. | C: license evidence-producing mechanism to operators | Software/agency integrator pays for a productive capability and current verification, not individual deliverables | License + usage receipts + independently measured service quality | Requires transferable proof, adoption and support; competitor can build or use free open source. Test one interoperable, source-verifiable external integration before extrapolating licenses. |

**Why B first:** The canonical native service already executes independently checkable CSV reconciliation. A contracted recurring outcome avoids the most extreme per-job distribution volume while permitting later qualified reuse. Candidate A remains a control; C is a possible successor, not a prerequisite. The objective is maximum *collected complete-cost profit* against competent alternatives—not an AI cost-per-token contest.

**External anchors, not simulated buyer data:** SaaS Capital's August 2026 private B2B SaaS survey (>1,000 companies) reports a median annual contract value of $24,266 (https://www.saas-capital.com/blog-posts/what-is-the-average-deal-size-for-private-saas-companies/). The separate 2026 bootstrapped $3M–$20M ARR benchmark reports median NRR 103% and GRR 91% (https://www.saas-capital.com/blog-posts/benchmarking-metrics-for-bootstrapped-saas-companies/). These are non-equivalent SaaS populations, not forecasts for our autonomous service. A $250k annual complete-service contract must earn its own evidence of willingness to pay and account scope.

## Mathematical scale gate, not a forecast

Annual $1B complete-cost profit requires, for example:
- 50 million accepted collected orders annually at $20 net/order;
- 10,000 active customer-years at $250k annual collected spend and **40% fully costed profit margin**;
- 4,000 active customer-years at $500k collected spend and **50%** net margin;
- 2,000 active customer-years at $1M collected spend and **50%** net margin.

These arithmetic conditions do **not** assert such contracts, margins or acquisition capacity exist. Aggregate contract-years are not a substitute for actual native accepted services, recognized revenue, reserves, collection timing, and independent verification. The initial $100k is one native account; do not copy it across matched arms and add the balances together.

## Proper game and operating controller

At each qualified buyer/partner opportunity, the enterprise can quote, not bid, license, bundle, partner, defer, construct a missing instrument or hold cash. The buyer compares value, price, risk, human+AI, other AI, self-service and refusal. A channel partner compares revenue share with its own sales, reputational exposure and contract costs. Suppliers quote actual terms and capacity. Seller decisions use only observable history and registered risk assumptions—never private reservation utility or future simulated draws.

Maximize expected *collected* incremental contribution (with a declared robust downside region), adjusted for repeat revenue and cost of acquisition, verification, service, refunds, outstanding obligations and capital delay. Do not describe one conditional quote as universal game-theoretic optimum.

**Finance:** The native account is the only authoritative enterprise financial ledger. Before any funded step, cover current obligations, refund exposure, taxes, permanent tails where applicable and working capital. Buyer outside income is **not** enterprise cash. Customer deposits are liabilities until earned according to contract. Source, authorization and state must survive retry/restart without duplicated receipts or seed refills.

## Executable next economic experiment

1. Use **the existing canonical run_world and native audit**, not a separate business simulator. Keep a completed standard incumbent as control; use an independently source-frozen candidate registration. First quantify the fixed initial-buyer-budget obstruction (PR #68).
2. With opt-in income039, give customers periodic **authored outside budgets**, leaving voluntary choice and paid channel reach intact. Test high/low externally funded buyer-income regions, unchanged native opening capital, and no-sales/no-demand negative control. Audit income events and all buyer purchases. **This is an enabling budget condition only, not validation of market demand.**
3. Under separately qualified source changes, construct costed partner-generated *new* buyer cohorts and unique accepted data work, rather than duplicating purchased artifacts or incrementing only buyer counts. Retain control arms with equal acquisition resources and buyer alternatives. Use source-bound native account reservations and explicit partner shares.
4. Replace approximate offer economics with qualified complete contracts and an admissible pricing policy. Predeclare a 60-calendar-month horizon, strong alternatives, finite experiment resources, and adverse worlds. Continue checkpointed operation until the registered horizon or a justified stopping rule; independently audit each native state and calculate exact trailing-365-day profit, net cash, collections, and protection.
5. Retain only independently demonstrated capability, distribution and profit gains. Report first crossing if it occurs; otherwise report the bottleneck (demand, value/price, unique work, verification, cash, channel/supplier, competition, etc.) and preservation of capital. Do not re-label forward conditional scaling as native execution.

**Current decision:** Income039 demonstrates only an optional engineering mechanism for independently testable exogenous budget replenishment; it is not sufficient for high-ACV enterprise adoption, unbounded demand, or a validated $1B scale result. The next highest-value missing capability is qualified, costed *new buyer + new unique work* generation with actual voluntary acceptance, followed by pricing/retention evidence.
