# Strategic commercial rerun 094

Prospective registration, November 4, 2026. This is a **conditional research projection**, not an operating account or a native commercial qualification. Historical accounts and results remain unchanged. No funding, demand or accepted production is inferred from the user's business size.

## Question

Can funded acquisition and capacity, with strong strategic competition, outperform competent organic and cautious acquisition policies after all modeled costs? Which assumptions would be needed for $3 billion or $10 billion in trailing-year profit? These are milestones, not caps. No parameter is selected because it crosses them.

The predecessor paid-reach experiment demonstrated a conditional financial loop on a small authored cohort. This comparison replaces its fixed 24 prospects and two purchase dates with a declared market population, heterogeneous budgets, opposing profit incentives, delays and correlated uncertainty. It does not retrospectively change that experiment.

## Market and uncertainty

The historical US Census population contains 6,281,253 employer firms below 100 employees, 93,341 with 100–499 and 21,041 with 500+. These are firms, **not qualified buyers**. Each projection represents one enterprise serving disjoint size bands, not pooled independent businesses.

Eligibility is hypothesized at 10%, with four analytical-work opportunities per eligible firm-year. Buyer values, per-opportunity budgets, quality, conversion, autonomy, resource envelopes and repeat/referral behavior remain authored hypotheses, disclosed in SOURCES.md and the executable constants. The model does not implement identified persistent buyer wallets. Its finite US market is a declared domain, not a universal commercial ceiling.

Run central, cost/value stress, aggressive rival, slow payment and zero demand separately. No empirical probabilities are assigned to these environments. The cost/value stress doubles unit resource costs, reduces values to 60% and acceptance to 80%. The aggressive rival has higher quality and a 40% lower unit cost. Slow payment increases procurement to three periods, collection to four and bad debt to 2% of revenue. Zero demand removes eligibility. Preserve every loss.

Engineering seeds are 94001–94004. Confirmation seeds are 94100–94123. Key each random stream by seed, month and purpose, pairing policies on common latent market and quality shocks. Generate realization only after a policy chooses. There is no previously favorable warm prefix. Aggregate binomial cohorts are projection units, not millions of rendered or independently accepted deliverables.

## Capital and objective

Compare $100,000 reference equity with separate $1 million, $100 million and $1 billion initial-equity scenarios. Equity arrives at time zero and is never revenue or profit. There are no later top-ups, debt, pooled accounts or invented credit. This is not a claim that financing is available.

Measure after-tax operating profit and investor opportunity cost separately. A hypothetical 15% annual book-equity hurdle is not an underwriting decision, liquidation cash or an investor agreement. Report every capital arm. Do not select a capital amount after confirmation and present that selection as validated optimal financing.

Non-entry is an explicit arithmetic comparator: no operations, costs or profit, with initial capital retained. It does not earn the investor hurdle automatically.

## Strategies and exact game boundary

All three policies use the same public information and competitive pricing game:

- **Organic:** no paid acquisition.
- **Staged:** choose among affordable absolute acquisition budgets up to 1% of free cash.
- **Adaptive:** choose among affordable absolute acquisition budgets up to free cash.

Absolute budgets are 1, 2 or 5 times powers of ten, starting at $100. The grid expands with available funds; it is not a universal scale cap. Higher-capital firms retain lower spending choices. Each candidate must also fund delivery reserves and prepaid capacity. All policies can decline negative expected contribution. This is a myopic budget-policy comparison, not a globally optimal dynamic enterprise strategy.

For each candidate, the seller commits to a price multiplier before a strong incumbent rival chooses its profit-maximizing response, including withdrawal. Buyers choose the best budget-feasible positive surplus among both providers, competent human-only work, human-plus-AI, self-service and no purchase. Outside options also obey budgets. Ties with nonparticipation decline. No forced costly copying or cooperation is assumed.

Price multipliers are 0, 0.05, every 0.005 from 0.10 through 0.50, then 0.60, 0.70, 1.00 and 1.40. Exhaustively evaluate every pair and resolve rival best-response ties against the seller. Exact optimality means zero enumeration gap **within this one-period finite commitment game and public distribution**. It is not continuous-price optimality, Nash equilibrium, annual equilibrium or optimal actual business. Grid refinement and profitable off-grid deviations are reported. First-mover commitment and a short-run unconstrained incumbent are explicit assumptions; the rival's full financing and entry dynamics are not solved.

Public scenario conditions are known planning inputs, not hidden labels revealed to the policy. Private realizations and future random draws are unavailable. Expected payoffs integrate the clipped common-demand and acceptance distributions instead of pretending clipping preserves their original means.

## Production, liquidity and costs

Model periods last exactly 365/12 days. Sixty periods span five model years. Report trailing twelve-period profit at periods 12, 24, 36, 48 and 60, never annualize a short cohort.

Pay development and setup of $50,000 at entry. The $2,000 monthly sales-platform resource envelope is a prepaid service, not invented employee labor. If it cannot be paid, new entry closes permanently; existing contracted deliveries continue under their funded production/support envelopes. Closed entry does not resume sales-platform spending.

Acquisition uses segment contact prices of $50/$500/$5,000, with a 30%/30%/40% allocation and 10% overlap. Integer rights are bought before decisions; fractional leads cannot produce orders. Capacity slots are dated and prepaid, at 5% of unit resource cost multiplied by one plus contacts divided by reference capacity 1,000/100/10. This convex supplier-congestion curve has no fixed universal throughput ceiling. Its parameters are hypotheses. Unused capacity expires. Orders cannot exceed contracted slots.

Reserve full production cost before accepting an order. Base procurement takes two periods; accepted output creates net recognized receivables, not cash. Base payment follows two periods later, with 23% delayed one additional period. Rejection, refunds, bad-debt haircuts, retries, acquisition failures, capacity, development, tools, support and maintenance are included in stated modeled envelopes. Actual full lifecycle resource usage and billing remain unmeasured.

Recognize a 26.5% modeled corporate-tax provision on positive profit after loss carryforward. Unpaid tax is a separate payable, never a funded reserve. Stop new entry on a tax shortfall, preserve it, and pay arrears from later free collections. Never spend negative cash. Record funded production reserves, tax payables, receivables, cash, available liquidity, accrual profit and the cash-reconciliation identity separately. Outstanding future production is a commitment, not an already incurred expense or free terminal value.

## Engineering lessons before confirmation

The initial draft had a coarse price floor that excluded profitable rival undercuts, mutable random streams that broke pairing, fractional planned contacts that could never execute, and a weak fixed-price comparator. Independent review found these before confirmation. The corrected implementation uses the stronger controls, refined grid, integer rights and keyed streams above. These corrections cannot be claimed as commercial discoveries or proof of market realism.
