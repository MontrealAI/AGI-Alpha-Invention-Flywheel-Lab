## Consequential possibility and competing complete arrangements

Question: can shared construction of an explicitly verified interoperable artifact make complete multi-party adaptation economically feasible when no individual participant benefits enough to fund the fixed core alone? A common adapter plus independently accepted buyer-specific adapters could lower coordination cost and permit new useful exchange across incompatible local formats. The economic scale and any actual unmet demand are UNKNOWN; no industry or superhuman advantage is presumed.

Compare three materially different, complete arrangements and no-deploy: **separate** buyer-funded standalone artifact development (each pays full fixed + own variable costs); **ordinary pool**, a competent conventional cost-sharing cooperative with the exact same production core and buyer access; **collective**, a conditional shared construction and verified shared-core allocation. **No-deploy** wins when real contracts/funds, rights or acceptance are missing. A conventional pool tie defeats exceptional mechanism advantage, even if simulated collective production is feasible. The actual seller may not book a simulated pledge as native collected income.
