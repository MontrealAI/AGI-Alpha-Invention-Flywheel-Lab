## Mechanism and decision

**Observed falsification:** The original `ImpactRegistry.evaluate` treated a matching requirement SHA-256 as sufficient evidence that an *old* affirmative review was current. On A→B→A and active→retired→active, the prior `MET` returned after restoration. The independent ordinary chronological replay discarded that finding, identifying a correctness flaw in source-version approval semantics. This is not evidence that a real government system was compromised.

**Prospective correction:** The same immutable existing events are retained. The evaluator computes the latest explicit update sequence for each requirement and accepts only reviews **after** that change, with a current requirement digest; an ordinary chronological scan remains a comparator. Unchanged requirements retain their approvals; new reviews after rollback work. The hash chain still requires an externally pinned correct tip to make tampering detectable. There is no signing-key migration, history rewrite, operating-account access or weakening of guards.

**Applicability and limits:** This is a reusable version-validity pattern for documents, rules and approvals *only when change events are complete, source-bound and trusted*. If the real policy source changes without an entered update, or if legal mapping, reviewer identity, audit-tip storage, source document authenticity, multi-tenant security or deployment control is absent, this prototype cannot establish compliance. Existing evidence digest strings in these cases refer to synthetic fixture assertions, not independently retrieved external evidence. No claim of audited legal compliance, TRL5, unique invention or beyond-ordinary algorithmic superiority. The strongest conventional solution can apply the same epoch rule.
