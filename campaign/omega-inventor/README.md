## What the new loop actually does

The constructor searches mappings from unfamiliar invoice/payment columns into a typed, executable reconciliation procedure. It receives source rows and declared units, not the checker's expected answers. Each proposed pruning/caching procedure is executed. A separate existing checker independently verifies normalized inputs, useful outputs, complete scope and justified refusal. No generated Python, shell commands or arbitrary tool effects are evaluated.

The development search evaluates 32 procedures on 16 inputs. It freezes a selected procedure before generating the 72 registered confirmation inputs. The updated procedure is compared with its predecessor, the existing conventional constructor and equal-content ordinary reuse. The learned object is a discovery procedure, not merely one solved mapping; its available predicates and interpreter nevertheless remain developer-authored.

The native bridge creates one explicitly fresh USD100,000 simulated `JournalAccount`. It prepays declared development costs, presents a modeled voluntary offer against a profit-seeking rival and outside options, executes an actual adapter, independently checks its outcome, binds that outcome to the admitted service, and settles through the existing native account. The bridge does not replace the ledger. Complete modeled costs include setup, development, failed qualification, delivery, checking, support, payment, maintenance, successor expenditure and tax. These are authored tariffs, not observed bills. The delivery contract prepays a 1,000-operation envelope; it does not claim that the measured search reduction automatically lowers the invoice.

A USD50 successor budget is spent only from native unrestricted earnings and actually executes a fresh source adapter. This demonstrates **funded by**, not **uniquely enabled by**: remaining seed or a competent ordinary method could also support the work. Pricing, market assumptions and this research allocation are not themselves learned from real customers or proved globally optimal.
