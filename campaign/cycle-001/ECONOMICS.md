## Conditional price/participation frontier

For a prospectively frozen horizon, define: N admitted offers (include all rejects/nonparticipants), q accepted-and-funded participation fraction, r expected refund/reversal fraction among accepted orders, p price, F allocated fixed lifecycle cost, cO cost per admitted offer, cA non-refund variable cost per accepted order, cR additional handling cost per refunded order, and T other admitted horizon/terminal costs. Define cR to exclude refunded principal because the revenue term is already net of refunds.

`Pi = N*q*(1-r)*p - F - N*cO - N*q*cA - N*q*r*cR - T`.

For N>0, q>0 and r<1, positive operating surplus requires:

`p > [F/N + cO + q*cA + q*r*cR + T/N] / [q*(1-r)]`.

This is a conditional algebraic frontier, not a fitted demand curve. If costs depend on q, r or p, retain those functions in the inequality. If q=0 or r=1, this revenue model cannot cover positive costs. Price, q, r, costs and the completed-service contract currently remain unmeasured. Correlated refunds require joint downside scenarios and funded reserves; their principal must not be counted both as netted revenue and an expense.

An upper feasible p additionally requires independent buyer evidence against equal-tool/free alternatives, switching friction, trust and residual risk. Savings predicted by the instrument create no automatic royalty. There is no defensible numerical price frontier until those measurements exist.
