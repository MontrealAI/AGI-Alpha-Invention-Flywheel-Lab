## What is actually constructed

The deliverable contains an editable design input, a compiler selecting a current-reversal schedule within declared current/power limits, a raw acquisition/calibration schema, a conventional conservative interval reconstruction, a separate Kirchhoff forward generator, independent acceptance checks, a separately constructed direct-agent comparator, and explicit obstacle-reopening logic. User-editable source permits inspection and modification; third-party software retains its own licenses. No proprietary customer document or third-party IP is transferred.

The compiler selects the largest current from 0.25, 0.5 and 1 mA permitted by the declared 1 mA limit and 1 mW specimen power envelope at 40 ohms. It emits three reversal pairs at that current, half and one quarter. These are numerical design conditions, not verified physical safety limits or permission to actuate equipment. High-impedance sensing is idealized. Hardware compliance, self-heating, lead thermoelectric behavior, finite input impedance, geometry and calibration require physical validation.

The proposed composition uses mature four-terminal/current-reversal analysis. The strong conventional control receives the identical complete acquisition, calibration and interval analysis; it is intentionally the same kernel, not a weakened comparison. A separate direct-agent implementation was constructed from the interface and task without inspecting the proposed code or hidden outcomes. It uses exact-rational interval arithmetic and an additional conservative nominal-current feasibility witness. That witness can abstain on valid data where current corrections are essential. All controls receive identical inputs and bounds. This is not an executed human-versus-AI productivity experiment, a population comparison of models or evidence of scientific novelty.

## Measurement and uncertainty contract

Each session supplies paired positive/negative current readings, two-terminal voltage, interior four-terminal voltage, voltage error bound, current error bound, positive gain interval and positive sense-spacing fraction interval. Offsets may be unknown but stable inside a session. Variable errors must fit the declared voltage envelope; unknown hidden drift is not magically detected. The estimator receives observations/calibration only, never truth class, seed, latent resistances or nuisance labels.

The independent forward implementation solves a five-node grounded resistor-chain Kirchhoff system. The analysis does not call it. Bulk resistance is uniform; sense fraction is near one half. Two-terminal resistance includes bulk plus aggregate contacts. Current reversal removes a stable additive offset. Exact decimal-rational interval propagation retains gain, geometry, current and voltage uncertainty. JSON endpoints are rounded outward; classifications follow the delivered conservative intervals. Overlapping change intervals return ambiguity. Unknown gain and geometry can make the physical question unidentifiable; additional computation alone is not a remedy.

Sample change threshold: absolute difference exceeds 0.5 ohm. Contact change threshold: absolute difference exceeds 0.25 ohm. An interval fully inside the corresponding symmetric threshold is unchanged; entirely outside is changed; otherwise ambiguous. These thresholds are an authored numerical decision contract, not a customer requirement or calibrated industrial tolerance.

## Cases and preregistered acceptance

Four truth classes: unchanged, contact-only, sample-only, both. Three nuisance families: noise, geometry uncertainty, and missing/drifting channels. Twelve replicates per cell, 144 cases per arm, 432 arm-case outputs. Development and confirmation use separate deterministic seed roots 970000 and 980000, each plus 1000 times truth index, 100 times family index and replicate 0–11. Cases are authored synthetic draws, not empirical samples or a proof for every continuous specimen.

Bulk baseline is Uniform(10,30) ohms, contacts Uniform(2,8). Nonzero sample change magnitude is Uniform(3,5), alternating sign by replicate. Nonzero contact magnitude is Uniform(1,2), with odd replicates negative at half magnitude. Actual sense fraction is Uniform(0.4999,0.5001), gain Uniform(0.9998,1.0002). Ordinary declared intervals are fraction [0.4998,0.5002], gain [0.9995,1.0005], voltage error 0.2 microvolt, current error 1 nanoampere. Stable offsets are independently Uniform(-2,2) millivolts.

Geometry replicates 8–11 instead declare fraction [0.35,0.65]. Channel replicates 0–3 omit the sense voltage; 4–7 use a declared 3 mV variable-error envelope; 8–11 retain ordinary calibrated conditions. These 48 hard cases remain in the denominator and may legitimately abstain. The other 96 cases form the declared tighter-envelope subset, not a post-hoc selection.

All reported intervals must enclose true values and differences; no confident false classification is allowed in any of 144 cases. At least 90% of the 96 tighter-envelope cases must fully classify correctly for each arm. Kirchhoff residual must be at most 1e-12 A. Every registered case/arm must be present exactly once. Missing inputs, invalid calibration, inconsistent reversal pairs and mutated classifications/intervals are tested. All arms and failures are retained; no favorable-seed selection or tolerance revision after confirmation.

Confirmation starts only after source/protocol publication and independent development review. Source hashes, output hashes and elapsed time are retained. A failed registered run remains failed; repairs require a new source-bound edition.

## Frontier Replay boundary

`replay_missing_sense` stores prior/current observation identities and reopens the specific missing-sense obstacle only when new sense observations are supplied. Renaming a tool or rerunning a solver without that observation does not reopen it. The same new information is available to the conventional control. Unit tests use synthetic newly supplied observations, not actual sensor improvement or autonomous scientific discovery. Broader tool-triggered research remains future work.
