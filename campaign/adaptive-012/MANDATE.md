## 2. Optimize the business, not the benchmark.

The goal is repeated, profitable, fully autonomous delivery and expansion in an explicitly simulated market. Fixed AI capabilities are sufficient for commercial success.

A conditional simulated commercial flywheel succeeds when accepted deliveries generate settled simulated revenue, complete-cost surplus and available earned funds that finance further profitable deliveries. Live customers, bank receipts, improved intelligence and invention superiority are not prerequisites for this verdict.

A successful retreat protects capital but does not, by itself, establish a commercial flywheel. Demonstrate continued profitable operation within a defensible opportunity set. Do not require every service, customer or adverse world to be profitable.

Maintain separate verdicts for commercial operation, competitive adaptation, invention and productive expansion. Do not make all four a global pass condition.

## 3. Make the complete offer the decision unit.

Jointly choose the customer problem, segment, qualification rule, promised outcome, delivery method, evidence, service terms, acquisition channel, capacity and price.

Implement a typed, executable strategy record containing:

- Customer problem and promised complete outcome.

- Price, payment, renewal and remedy policies.
- Delivery deadlines and support obligations.
- Acquisition channel and customer-context requirements.
- Capacity, upfront cash, later costs and liability limits.
- Applicability conditions, provenance and falsifiable hypotheses.
Strategy generation must change executable behavior. It cannot invent faster delivery without changing production, or declare reduced risk without improving verification, remedies or failure rates.

Search for unmet demand and barriers to completion, not only familiar job titles. An offer may become valuable because it removes uncertainty, integration effort, delay or repeated customer work. Model that benefit through delivered attributes and buyer decisions, not a free “differentiation multiplier.”

## 4. Diagnose before discounting.

When orders or profits disappoint, distinguish competing explanations: wrong customer segment, insufficient demand, unclear proof, inadequate output, excessive buyer effort, slow delivery, poor payment terms, excessive price, costly acquisition or constrained capacity.

Before each experiment, record the decision it can change, competing explanations, predicted observations, the smallest discriminating test, complete cost, maximum exposure and continue/repair/switch rule.

Choose experiments for decision value relative to cost and delay. Do not run elaborate simulations that cannot change an operating decision. Do not infer “no business” from one offer, one segment or one fixed strategy.

Use source-backed evidence where available and explicit plausible ranges otherwise. Missing live observations should lead to bounded experiments, not universal HOLD.

## 7. Capture value with strategic premium pricing.

Use competent human-delivered complete service as the primary commercial benchmark, at realistic human speeds and loaded costs for equivalent obligations.

Start near comparable total human service value/cost, then optimize price and win probability jointly for sustainable collected profit. Do not automatically give away AI savings, impose a fixed discount or use AI cost plus a small markup.

Consider premium segments, differentiated outcomes, recurring contracts and bundles. Larger discounts are allowed when they produce better complete economic returns, not because the enterprise must win every job.

Include relevant AI-assisted alternatives and competition as actual market choices. Compare full outcomes: a software subscription still may require customer preparation, operation, checking and support.

Use adaptive search with boundary and resolution checks. Report optimality only within the tested information, model and action space. Do not confuse a precise numerical answer with empirical certainty.

## 8. Make growth decisions endogenous.

Model reachable opportunities, lead quality, channel capacity, sales cycles, activation, repeat frequency and churn. New channels and segments may be discovered and entered through explicit costed actions. They must not deliver unlimited customers for free.

Test expansion within existing customer relationships before assuming every job needs a newly acquired customer. Deduplicate customer demand, shared onboarding and bundle/component revenue. Measure whether cross-selling and context reuse improve incremental economics.

Treat market and capacity limits as stated assumptions with sensitivity ranges and possible investment responses. A projection that misses $1 billion under one policy is not proof that all policies fail.

There is no artificial service-count or revenue ceiling. Simulate time to revenue milestones, collected profit, cash survival and capital needs. Distinguish run rate, realized annual revenue, cumulative revenue and available cash. Scale calculations must remain connected to delivery capacity, demand and obligations.
