## Decision

The most useful immediately executable candidate is **a scoped recovery drill backed by a jointly feasible capacity commitment**, followed by a different planned migration/reconfiguration deliverable using the retained recovery procedure. This combines an ordinary valuable service with a potentially useful coordination instrument. Select it for near-term experimental decision value and accessible synthetic acceptance, **not** because demand, novelty, a defensible advantage or superiority has been established.

The central falsifiable proposition is: *with explicit supplier commitments, shared resources, full costs and buyer outside options, at least one complete recovery arrangement can be delivered and collected while preserving its other commitments; collected unrestricted earnings can then finance a distinct accepted successor*. The current environment can test that proposition only in a declared synthetic world. A local recovered toy application provides real computational output inside that simulation; it provides no evidence of recovery on customer infrastructure.

Do not select an ordinary plan-checker as the outcome. The accepted first deliverable must include actual restoration of synthetic application state and behavior, measured against requirements frozen before execution. Capacity-checking is one dependency of that delivery.

## Voluntary terms and resource boundaries

**A: scoped drill.** Buyer supplies authorized synthetic inputs, required restored state, functional tests, deadline and maximum total willingness to pay. Provider supplies an explicit restoration procedure and reserved execution slot. Buyer can select an incumbent route or refuse. Payment is due only under the frozen acceptance contract; deposits remain refundable liabilities until earned. Failure triggers the registered correction/refund/dispute branch. No claims about production security or real backup completeness are implied.

**B: capacity pool.** Suppliers declare named resource identities, availability intervals, setup/processing time, region/shared-dependency tags, reservation price, cancellation terms and which incident scenarios their promise covers. Overlapping commitments share one authoritative capacity account. Buyer commitments are conditional on an atomic feasible basket; a capacity checker is not supplier consent. Correlated failures are tested jointly. A plan may decline a buyer or charge for genuinely additional capacity rather than silently assuming independence. Opportunity cost of idle reserved slots belongs in the model.

**C: batteries.** Pack owner permits diagnosis and transfer; qualified tester specifies scope and uncertainty; integrator accepts compatibility and installation; buyer accepts a complete system; financier pays only at declared milestones. In the absence of these participants, there is no binding deal. Diagnosis, transport, storage, rejected packs, sorting, repurposing, integration, certification, installation and residual warranty obligations all count.

**D: pooled batch.** Each buyer states quantity, quality and deadline plus willingness to proceed only if the whole batch meets its condition. Machine owners commit actual capacity under identity and timing constraints. Setup cost and failure allocation are agreed before financing. A canceled buyer may invalidate the basket; previously incurred setup costs remain expenses rather than vanishing. Same-site power loss can invalidate several nominally separate suppliers.

## Smallest decisive registered experiment

1. Freeze the original objective, acceptance, source, inputs, funding ledger, full cost assumptions, hidden-event generation and comparison before running outcomes. Label fresh account and every financial record SIMULATED.
2. Start exactly US$100,000 in the canonical native account. No debt, new equity, top-up or historical-account migration in the primary case. Freeze a finite case set containing positive demand, no purchase, cheaper incumbent, delayed buyer response, delayed payment, nonpayment, failed restore, retry and correlated shared-provider outage.
3. Offer a scoped restore to buyers with finite budgets and explicit complete outside options. Acquisition/rejection costs are incurred even if no deal results. Never use the hidden outcome/valuation while selecting the offer.
4. Reserve the necessary restoration slots and maximum authorized cash exposure atomically before a promise. Introduce two overlapping requests to the same supplier resource and independently verify that no accepted basket can double-sell it. A supplier refusal is not overridden by the optimizer.
5. Restore a local synthetic application from a frozen backup. Independent acceptance checks restored data hashes, independently defined semantic queries/behaviors, the agreed deadline, and disclosed data-loss tolerance. Rejected output receives no accepted-delivery claim.
6. Record invoice separately from receivable, collection, revenue recognition, taxes, refund reserve and unrestricted cash. Actual payment processing is absent; settlement events are explicitly modeled.
7. Before revealing the successor's task details, freeze a budget and a different useful outcome (e.g. planned application migration to a new schema, or a manufacturing batch calendar). Authorize it only from collected earned cash after all existing reserves/obligations. Record deferral if unaffordable. Seed could have afforded the same project; earnings-funded is not earnings-enabled unless the counterfactual establishes it.
8. Compare retained method with a competent direct route given the same information, tools, resources and ordinary reusable content. Include preparation, execution, verification and correction. If both use the same established scheduler or restore procedure, a tie is likely and acceptable. No autonomous invention or learning advantage follows merely from reuse.
9. Give the challenger source, requirements, all adverse cases, native events, full costs and exact claim. Have a separate adjudication reproduce objections. Add fresh prospective cases after any repair; keep the original failed cases and incurred expense.

## Cost contract and sensitivity

Use resource accounting rather than a price chosen to force profit. AWS's public infrastructure prices are a floor on particular components, not the complete cost of a competent managed service. Public wage data is a reference for a human alternative, not proof of task time or an invoice.

| Cost category | Required treatment in the selected simulation |
|---|---|
| Research, development, rejected experiments | One-time incurred scenario expense before first sale, with source/provenance; do not amortize it away from the first-turn claim. |
| Unsuccessful acquisition | Charge every contact/qualification attempt, including refusal and nonpurchase; no guaranteed lead count. |
| Onboarding and buyer coordination | Explicit hours/resource events; customer participation separate from internal human supervision. |
| Production, inference and tools | Charge measured local resources where measurable; model/tool economic cost remains an explicitly assumed envelope. Included Pro execution is not zero economic cost. |
| Infrastructure and capacity reservation | Charge reserved idle time, active time, data storage/transfer and shared-dependency backups. |
| Human supervision and expert review | If used in AI operation, label hybrid and account for hours. A strict autonomous arm cannot invent a mandatory human employee merely to inflate costs, or omit actual intervention. |
| Failures, retries, support, maintenance | Charge each incurred event; freeze support/maintenance horizon and retain unresolved tails. |
| Refunds, disputes and bad debt | Separate reductions in revenue/refund liabilities, collection loss and handling expense; avoid double counting. |
| Financing | Primary case assumes no debt or equity beyond opening seed. Borrowing or extra equity must not appear as revenue or profit in any later separately registered arm. |
| Taxes | Use a clearly authored scenario rate until a real entity/jurisdiction is established. No legal tax estimate is supplied here. |
| Actual host/model bills, insurance, real buyer acquisition, supplier quotes | UNKNOWN. Do not fill with zero; report conditional simulated totals and the maximum additional unknown cost the observed margin could absorb. |

Useful wage anchor retrieved on 2026-11-05: U.S. BLS reports May 2025 information-security-analyst median annual wage US$129,180. Dividing by an explicitly assumed 2,080 paid hours gives about US$62.11/hour base wage. An illustrative 1.3–1.8 loading multiplier yields US$80.74–111.79/hour, **but those loading factors and all task-hours are scenario assumptions, not measured market quotes**. For a professional-service sensitivity only, not measured SQLite task duration, vary onboarding plus acceptance review at 0.5/2/8 hours, supplier reservation cost at 1×/2×/5× its registered value, failed acquisition contacts at 1/5/20 and settlement at 2/15/45 days. A competent human conventional comparator can reuse SQLite scripts and machine-speed checks; it must not be modeled as manually repeating each row operation. Human-plus-AI and self-service alternatives require their own hours, software charges and coordination burden. Do not compare AI to full wage cost while calling its preparation and supervision free.

Before claiming profitable repeated volume, require accepted demand, finite capacity, observed acquisition/retention, error/support rates and working-capital requirements. No source in this review supports a US$3 billion scale extrapolation. A small positive simulated cohort cannot be annualized as that target.
