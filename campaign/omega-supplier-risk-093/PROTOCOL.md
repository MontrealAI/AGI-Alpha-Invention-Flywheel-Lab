## Consequential question
Can rearranging a fixed paid-distribution invoice into contingent supplier compensation, with identical buyers and production, make incremental expansion **voluntarily profitable for both seller and supplier in every registered adverse environment**? If not, which missing information/contracting/production means would actually need construction before a paid-channel bet?

## Four eligible arrangements
1. **Organic/current complete seller**: published costed native seller with no paid channel (existing strong incumbent in the narrow same-menu experiment).
2. **Prepaid native pilot**: original paid supplier purchase, contact and renewal policy; compare source-recorded after-tax profit, reserves, collections and adverse regions.
3. **Contingent supplier**: a *non-executed contract hypothesis*, paying a nonnegative fee only after verified incremental outcomes. Supplier cost, outside option, willingness, activation, attribution and customer response are **unknown**; no action may be funded or declared feasible merely from algebra. We test only a **fee-removal upper bound**, assuming the original paid contacts, failed quotes, production, acceptance, settlement, displacement and other costs magically remain identical. This is an ex post relaxation, not a new simulated native account or causal result.
4. **No entry/no deploy**: zero new profit, zero spending; not a way to erase already-incurred costs in running businesses.

## Computation and frozen assertions

```
(pilot profit + pilot modeled tax) - (organic profit + organic modeled tax) + pilot paid-reach invoice.
```
The invoice removal is a best-case **upper bound** on otherwise unchanged seller margin before paying any contingent fee; it is NOT an actual feasible supplier offer, because supplier outside options and operations are not modeled. A positive risk-transfer payment only transfers value, never creates joint surplus. Under identical other activities, if the bound is negative, no nonnegative contingent supplier fee makes the seller weakly better off than organic. If the supplier incurs any net cost in an environment with zero delivered benefit, positive bilateral participation additionally fails absent a new signal/subsidy/means. No assumptions about additional tax in the unexecuted contract; quote the pre-tax upper bound only.
