# AGI ALPHA — 25-job portfolio and strategic autonomous pricing

**BLUE SKY · Beyond-Human Foresight · Profitable fixed-capability autonomous delivery**

## Decision and evidence boundary

Select 25 digital-service job categories for low-friction autonomous execution and independently testable acceptance. This is a **ranked research portfolio**, not 25 validated paying markets or a forecast of sales. Scores, non-RFP human task hours, AI resource budgets, buyer-choice parameters, onboarding costs and simulated conversion are explicit author-assigned planning inputs. The ranking is neither statistically calibrated nor an empirically determined optimal portfolio.

**First experiment lanes:** (1) bounded software bug fix with independently executable tests, (2) QA regression test construction, (3) authorized technical RFP response plus evidence packet. The RFP lane has the largest specific sourced workload benchmark (33h per RFP); code/QA lanes have stronger automated acceptance, but buyer acquisition and market economics are not established for any lane. Separately compare a vendor security questionnaire, source-bound KPI report and ETL QA as alternatives. Do not replace the active owner implementation in PR #16.

Human comparison is a *competent human-delivered service* at loaded wages (June 2026 US private-industry aggregate benefit factor); human labor is a lower-bound proxy, not a published vendor retail quote. AI workflows include autonomous onboarding, source acquisition, production, quality verification, correction, client handoff, development, and support. Charge model tokens, tools, compute, retries and real resource use. **No assumed human QA team.** Customer permissions, identity, legal signoff or required approval still impose genuine buyer friction and scoped boundaries.

## The economically correct pricing rule

Optimize the **probability-weighted contribution from the customer relationship**, not the probability of winning a single job. For each price and scoped job:

```text
qualified_quote_value(p) = P(win | p, buyer, rivals)
    × [ expected_repeat_deliveries(p) × (P(accepted_and_collected | p) × p
        − AI production / verification / support / risk resources)
        − autonomous onboarding resources if won ]
    − autonomous cost to qualify and quote the prospect
```

Select the price with the largest **positive expected value**, or **do not bid**. Calculate customer utility using accepted outcome, reliability, waiting time, trust, integration friction, switching cost, and the outside options of another autonomous provider, a capable human, doing it internally, or declining. Model rivals as optimizing their own profit and changing their prices; do not treat posted list prices as immovable. Account for collections, liabilities, reserves, refunds, customer retention and capacity before committing cash.

**What the current executable actually does:** an explicit *illustrative Stackelberg price-leader scenario*: for each of 51 candidate prices, a modeled rival chooses its profit-maximizing best response from 31 price candidates; buyers choose between sellers, human service, self-service and no purchase under assumed softmax/generalized-cost choices. A bounded repeat-purchase prior and three stress settings (baseline 50%, AI parity price war 30%, independently verified differentiation 20%) determine modeled expected collected contribution. The weights are not learned data. The resulting price is an optimizer **conditional on those assumptions, not a game-theoretic equilibrium theorem**. Do not claim a guaranteed win probability, and do not infer that the scenario 35% win rate is observed.

**Discount policy:** start customer discussions from a **verified comparable human-delivered service price**, not from AI cost plus markup. Then compare nearby discounts and higher prices, and optimize net lifetime return under learned buyer response and competitive moves. If real evidence demands a much larger discount, surface the price/value tradeoff rather than silently claiming it is "just enough". Current BLS wage proxies and assumed rival prices do not authorize any real quote. There is no universal 5% discount and no need to win every job. A high price with zero accepted demand is not a successful strategy.

## Adversarial calibration before real use

1. **First admitted cohort:** choose source-authorized, paid-service-eligible cases with explicit specs. Verify autonomous task success with independent checkers and actual tool/model cost receipts, including retries, edits, failures and support. Decline jobs requiring prohibited actions or unsupported attestations.
2. **Measure onboarding:** record agent tool calls and external resource expenses independently of buyer authorization/approval time. Keep a separate identity/access/setup-friction variable in buyer utility; zero human labor in our operation does not make enterprise sales friction disappear.
3. **Observe price choices responsibly:** record offers, accepted/rejected quotes, revisions, price points, competitor bids or credible substitutes, actual collection, churn, and downstream cost. Use consented, correctly scoped offer experiments; no coordinated pricing with competitors, hidden price discrimination, or false claims. Before enough observations exist, report uncertainty intervals rather than precise conversion probabilities.
4. **Update game/strategy:** fit a buyer-response and price-elasticity model on historical cohorts only. Validate against held-out prospects; check competitor best-response regret, repeat retention, equilibrium assumptions and capacity effects. Optimize incremental collected profit and sustainable funds, not gross volume. Keep stronger AI alternatives as realistic price constraints.
5. **Capital discipline:** rank opportunities by *observed* marginal unrestricted cash generated per acquisition effort, working capital and elapsed time, not these author scores. Preserve refund reserves, liabilities, quality failures and no-bid outcomes; do not reinvest simulated or pending funds as real cash.

## $1 billion arithmetic is not a revenue forecast

For any portfolio: `annual_revenue = sum(accepted_and_collected_jobs_i × collected_price_i)`; neither worldwide labor income nor generated leads establishes accepted demand. At the model-only RFP quote about $813, **$1B annual revenue would require roughly 1.23 million paid responses/year**. Using the Loopio survey average of 166 responses/organization/year as an optimistic upper-reuse assumption implies about 7,400 paying RFP customers; it is not a customer-acquisition forecast and those customers could procure fewer paid responses from us. A diverse 25-category portfolio lowers dependence on a single category but increases integration and distribution requirements.

## Reproduction

```sh
python3 -m unittest discover -s portfolio25 -p 'test_*.py' -v
python3 portfolio25/pricing.py --input portfolio25/jobs.json --output /tmp/agi-alpha-pricing-first-NEW.json
```

`jobs.json` has 25 explicit scoped service definitions, acceptance conditions, authority boundaries, weighted subjective ranks, wage sources and hypothetical agent resource assumptions. `reference_strategy_results.json` is the exact deterministic scenario record; it is not customer evidence. The script uses the Python standard library, no paid API, no credentials, no web access and does not touch any historical ledger or production PR. It refuses to overwrite an existing output. Every live validation gate remains distinct.
