## Finite opportunity comparison and choice
1. Autonomous site-specific brackish-water plants: replace constant electrical operation with flexible electrodialysis plus water buffering while preserving delivery and salinity obligations. Strong complete alternative is existing FCCC ED, flexible RO, water/battery storage, conventional engineering, or no deployment. Selected for measured public input and immediate falsifiable bridge; NOT claimed new ED physics.
2. Renewable industrial heat: replace electrical storage with thermal storage while preserving process temperature and heat flux. DOE documents this existing mechanism; material lifetime, integration/qualification and heat-transfer limits dominate. A heat ledger alone would not test those limits; not selected in this stage.
3. Renewable ammonia: shift hydrogen inventory and synthesis duty while preserving ammonia output. NREL's 2025 flexible-ammonia study establishes an existing complete modeling alternative; synthesis dynamics and hydrogen containment add validation scope. Not selected because this stage can more directly challenge water delivery with measured plant traces, not because ammonia capital is too high.

### Pre-confirmation clarification (independent verifier review)
Nonnegative spill is allowed and counted; do not assume counterfactual curtailment. Each hour's observed inflow is available before that hour's withdrawal: this certifies hourly balance only, not continuous reliability. Positive counter increments over gaps longer than15minutes are kept as unqualified-gap quantity, excluded from supply; resets never replenish supply. Constructor may return minimum integer identical parallel train count from observed daily quantity and its storage design; proportional identical-train production is a labeled deployment assumption, not measured scale-up. Source/API hashes bind dates but do not make shared public data a protected holdout.
Trace time is elapsed hourly time from the first recorded timestamp rounded down to its hour, reported explicitly as window_start_utc; it is NOT local-clock calendar demand. Demand is uniform. Counter increments spanning an hour boundary become available at the later observation (delayed availability), rather than fabricated interpolation. Missing initial/trailing observations are not evidence of zero production. The replay only constructs a conditional design for its recorded supply, never a guaranteed weather/site service. Any source checksum mismatch prohibits trace-based design.
Hourly supply/demand netting permits direct-through use within the hour: not all inflow is stored before withdrawal. Transient peak tank capacity, flow rating and subhour delivery remain unqualified. This aggregate convention is shared by the independent LP; zero tank for exactly matched hourly inflow/demand does not prove continuous service without a buffer.
