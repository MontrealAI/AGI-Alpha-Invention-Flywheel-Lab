## A documented formation obstacle

[NIST's May 2026 perspective, page 3](https://tsapps.nist.gov/publication/get_pdf.cfm?pub_id=959979) describes a reciprocal barrier in autonomous materials laboratories: bespoke engineering raises adoption costs, while fragmented demand discourages vendors from developing reusable interfaces. Its workshop example discusses X-ray diffractometer manufacturers. This is investigated industry evidence, not demand for GENESIS. The [NIST programme updated 30 October](https://www.nist.gov/programs-projects/development-standards-support-modular-and-autonomous-laboratory-ecosystem) distinguishes sample handling, communications, data and algorithm portability.

The proposed valuable capability is not another dashboard: construct a new experimental ability once and adapt it across qualified installations and scientific purposes without repeating nearly all development. This may require new measurement design, hardware, calibration, analysis or process construction. Similar equations or API compatibility do not prove physical transfer.

## Three competing complete arrangements

### A. Jointly commissioned scientific-computation capability

**Outcome:** make a decision-relevant simulation or computation practical at specified accuracy, scale, latency and total cost. **Roles:** independent scientific users, simulation providers and hardware providers; no identified buyers. **Technical hypothesis:** a new reduction or representation supports distinct tasks through economical, validated adapters. **Arrangement:** conditional purchases of accepted capability packages with shared core development and separate domain acceptance. **Rights:** preserve public-code licenses and customer confidentiality; public algorithms do not acquire royalties through registration. **Funding:** actual paid useful milestones or authorized capital, never conditional invoices as cash. **Decisive test:** construct a previously unspecified common method from natural task requirements, then evaluate fresh tasks and complete adaptation cost against specialist solvers, direct frontier agents and competent human teams with equal tools. A faster kernel alone does not establish a new market.

### B. Portable autonomous experimental capabilities — strongest documented formation lead

**Outcome:** buy a qualified experimental ability rather than repeatedly commission bespoke laboratory integration. **Roles:** independent materials R&D users, instrument vendors, an executing lab and separately authorized risk capital. **Technical hypothesis:** a restricted experiment-construction method, calibration contract and supported adapters meet different requirements without hidden per-client redevelopment. Thin films, powders and bulk samples cannot be assumed to share unchanged physical operations.

**Arrangement:** simultaneous conditional orders for specified outcomes, a common capability specification and customer-specific acceptance. Founder benefits may be agreed deployment credits or support priority, not invented investment returns. Agree reuse rights, maintenance, withdrawal and failure exposure. No coordinated downstream prices or compulsory native route. **Finance:** payment for a stage counts only if that stage is itself an accepted deliverable on disclosed terms; finance all remaining pre-receipt costs with real authorized resources. **Rights:** portable artifacts and supported interfaces; confidential customer results stay protected.

**Strong alternatives:** conventional lab engineering, cloud labs, vendor systems, existing EPICS/SiLA/experimental software and the [SPIRE effort announced 8 October](https://www6.slac.stanford.edu/news/2026-10-08-slac-leads-new-project-build-self-driving-laboratory-future). The generic shared-lab or standards-consortium idea is not new. The residual must be a concrete unserved scientific function or demonstrably better complete delivery, not a new platform name.

**Decisive test:** a first declared scientific function and a different fresh function on independently configured equipment, with actual calibration, acceptance, integration, support and lifecycle cost compared against ordinary construction/procurement using equal means. Simulation helps development but cannot qualify physical portability. No partner access, accepted price window or qualified transferable instrument is established.

### C. Adaptive production for variable reclaimed inputs

**Outcome:** deliver a specified material or component from varying reclaimed feedstock when a fixed accepted recipe is inadequate. **Roles:** feedstock suppliers, processors, equipment vendors and output customers. **Technical hypothesis:** a measurement-to-process method connects composition variation to processing and acceptance within a declared material family. NIST's paper identifies changing recycled input streams as an ongoing experimental challenge, not a guaranteed business. **Arrangement:** jointly specify feedstock, conversion, output purchases and reject liability. **Finance/rights:** cover sampling, failed conversion, processing, waste, qualification and working capital using authorized resources. **Decisive test:** a particular conversion with source-bound measurements, mass/energy balances and accepted output against current recycling, virgin inputs, industrial symbiosis and non-entry. Waste listings and equilibrium calculations cannot validate kinetics or manufacturing economics.

B has the closest documented match to technical-and-institutional complementarity. A is a strong competing route with more immediately accessible computational tests; C has a longer physical path. This is a reasoned research priority, not a proved optimal allocation or fixed industry catalogue. Actual residual requirements and obtainable evidence decide the next construction.

## Market thresholds become technical targets

The paper's authored model has expected contribution p*n*(q-d-f)-(K+A). With p=0.8, q=$300,000, d=$75,000 and f=A=0, positive contribution requires K < $180,000*n. The strict setup boundaries for one, two, three and four buyers are $180,000, $360,000, $540,000 and $720,000; equality gives zero. These are illustrative USD, not measured prices or costs.

The paper's four-buyer K=$600,000 case gives $120,000 expected contribution, $300,000 on success, $900,000 prefunding and a $600,000 loss on common technical failure. Four buyers do not diversify this common failure.

New analytical applications (not measured gains):

- Setup K=$500,000 allows three buyers to give $40,000 expected contribution, with $725,000 prefunding. This is a construction target, not a demonstrated AI cost reduction.
- Accepted price q=$250,000 with other base conditions unchanged gives four buyers -$40,000. Buyer alternatives improve with public AI too.
- Five buyers with common qualification probability 0.60 give $75,000, below the original four-buyer $120,000. At unchanged setup and per-buyer margin, fifth-buyer inclusion improves contribution only when common probability exceeds 0.64. These probabilities are sensitivity assumptions.
- A $40,000 per-delivery route burden makes the original case -$8,000. Native-route benefits must exceed full burdens; service success does not determine token price.

Thus neither the largest coalition nor the newest technology is automatically best. Choose technical scope, participants, terms, rights and funding sequence jointly. Do not add a buyer or assumed financing merely to close a favorable spreadsheet.
