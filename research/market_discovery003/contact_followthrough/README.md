An ordinary SciPy/HiGHS constructor meets the two nominal parent states with 36 contacts on a 24 mm square. Scaling all microscopic lengths and indentation by 1/k, then tiling k² copies in the same footprint, constructs 144- and 576-site variants. CSV family assignments and editable OpenSCAD geometries are generated by the program. This is a separate reimplementation of the inspected equations, not a rerun/requalification of the entire parent package.

With independent Hertz contact radius a=sqrt(R*d), area=pi*a² and normal force=(4E/3R)*a³, each miniature contact's area and force divide by k². Therefore total load, area and static/dynamic forces are preserved at the rescaled stroke. The independent normalized transport proxy 2*sum(a) and normal stiffness both multiply by k. Stroke divides by k. Established similarity and contact mechanics supply this construction; no novel law, solver or superior discovery policy is claimed.

This checks a model identity, not physical accuracy.

The screen solves M*q=1 with Mii=1/(2ai), Mij=1/(pi*rij), frozen Hertz radii, and normalized conductivity. It is an approximate paired-half-space point kernel, not a converged BEM or self-consistent elastic/thermal solution. No approximation-error bound is established. Film sensitivity uses (1+b)/(1/g+b) with assumed b=9. Actual film resistance is unknown. No measured W/K or A/V is predicted; the parent's soft-material shear coefficients cannot be silently transferred to conducting metals.

Thus 4x is not an achieved cooling gain. Elastic coupling, films, manufacturing tolerances, wear, stiffness and material realization can dominate. Original parent calibration/transfer failures remain open. The next consequential evidence is paired mechanics/transport validation for a real application, not another nominal optimum.
