# AGI ALPHA commercial baseline: autonomous frontier-AI enterprise versus human-delivered service

**BLUE SKY · Beyond-Human Foresight · Fully autonomous operations**  
**Benchmark revision: v2 — 30 October 2026**

## Primary comparison and correction

**Primary benchmark:** A super-competently configured autonomous frontier-AI operation **E** versus a competent human-delivered enterprise **H** at realistic human speed and fully loaded wages, with the **same required outcome and service obligations**.

**E is end-to-end autonomous.** AI agents perform planning, research, production, source checking, adversarial QA, tests, revisions, customer acquisition and proposals, delivery, support, software development and maintenance. Select the strongest *appropriate* currently available models, potentially escalating to premium frontier reasoning models and using validated deterministic tools. Optimize the combination of acceptance rate, speed, reliability and total cost, not the unit price of one prompt. **Do not impose hypothetical human validation hours or human development/sales/support staffing on E.**

Automating an activity does not eliminate the resources required to perform it. Charge actual model/tool calls, infrastructure, paid data, computer use, rework/failed attempts, paid customer distribution, transaction fees, warranty/refund risk, and amortized agent/tool development. AI-operated acquisition does not itself create buyers or make paid channels free. If a human is *actually* required by law, client contract, credential, or operational necessity, measure that cost in a separately labeled hybrid mode; **never pass off a partly human operation as fully autonomous**. If fully autonomous E cannot meet the fixed acceptance requirement, the task is unaccepted rather than silently escalated to unpaid human labor.

**H** includes research, execution, proofreading/review, delivery, support and corrections performed at human speed, with wages, benefits and necessary overhead. The examples below show **only loaded human labor**, making them an incomplete lower-bound *scenario* for full H operating cost, rather than a full human-business P&L. H and E must meet the *same* scope, quality, data rights, legal constraints, delivery standards, deadlines and refund/liability obligations.

The benchmark separates three claims:

1. **Human-to-autonomous-AI productivity:** cost, throughput, accepted quality and speed on the same job; the primary comparator is competent H, *not* the strongest current AI rival.
2. **A commercially viable AI-native enterprise:** actual or labeled simulated customer adoption, earned/collected prices, complete full-service costs and risk, versus human+AI hybrids, equally capable rival AI providers, free/internal alternatives, and no launch. AI competition tests attainable price and defensibility, **not** whether the technology can replace expensive human labor.
3. **The invention flywheel:** a verified innovation creates additional productive capability, accepted delivery yields net available earned resources, and inherited capabilities improve **fresh successor invention** versus matched no-inheritance and ordinary-reuse controls. This is a distinct, higher bar; do not block an initially viable AI-native service because its algorithm is not exclusive.

## Two distinct flywheels — commercial success does not require improving intelligence

**Commercial success is an independent operating achievement.** A business can repeatedly deliver accepted outcomes, collect actual revenue, pay all applicable costs and obligations, and use available earned funds to finance further profitable delivery **without improving its models, algorithms, tools, or invention capabilities**. This is a self-funding autonomous commercial flywheel. Merely having favorable estimated token costs is a promising unit-economics hypothesis, not a demonstrated operating flywheel.

- **Unit economics:** record accepted and collected service revenue, actual model/tool/computation/external resource expenses, every failed or refunded attempt, agent-performed acquisition, autonomous QA/revisions/support, paid platform services, amortized development and maintenance, taxes as applicable, contingency and liabilities. No default human-review hour; charge human escalations only when an actual service requires them.
- **Repeatability:** demonstrate multiple cycles of the same quality-accepted service under the **same capabilities**, recording demand, fulfillment capacity, acquisition expense, collections, refunds, reserves and cash after obligations. Operating profit without growth is already a profitable business; a self-funding delivery flywheel requires evidence that proceeds finance additional repeatable profitable work.
- **Autonomous commercial flywheel qualification:** positive net earnings after complete costs, collected cash sufficient to cover liabilities and reserves, and traceable use of available earned funds for subsequent accepted, profitable service. No new method, exclusive algorithm or AI-over-AI superiority is required. A human-work comparator establishes a cost/productivity opportunity; realistic AI competitors still affect sustainable sale price and demand.
- **Independent invention flywheel:** causal demonstration that a verified retained invention improves genuinely fresh successor discovery or construction against capable no-inheritance/ordinary-reuse controls after complete lifecycle costs. This is additional and optional for declaring the commercial flywheel successful, not a hidden condition of doing profitable autonomous work.
- **Strong transformational claim, separately:** a previously uneconomic activity crossing from negative to positive complete-cost economics against the strongest conventional route is an important scientific/strategic claim, but is **not** necessary to demonstrate a profitably operating autonomous business. A capable frontier-AI business can be profitable even when an existing human-delivered service was profitable before automation.

**Illustrative fixed-capability example:** a $95 *collected* article with a genuinely complete $5.03 autonomous service cost would yield $89.97 per accepted delivery. If independently verified and repeatable, those earnings could finance more deliveries with unchanged agent capabilities. The $95, $5.03, acceptance, demand, and subsequent reinvestment are illustrative assumptions, not observed transactions.

**Global opportunity is an exploration hypothesis, not captured revenue.** The World Bank reports approximately USD118.35 trillion of 2025 world GDP and the ILO reports a 52.6% global labor-income share. Their rough product is USD62 trillion in annual labor income; neither statistic identifies a USD40 trillion addressable market for full keyboard-and-mouse service automation. Screen-based work categories, autonomous completion rates, demand, price and capture must be measured bottom up. Sources: https://data.worldbank.org/indicator/NY.GDP.MKTP.CD?locations=1W-US and https://ilostat.ilo.org/blog/the-world-of-work-and-the-2030-agenda-a-ten-year-review/

**Report four independent verdicts:** `autonomous_unit_profitability`, `repeatable_self_funding_commercial_flywheel`, `capability_improvement_flywheel`, and `transformational_economic_boundary_crossing`. A GO for the second must not be downgraded to HOLD because the third or fourth is still UNKNOWN. Conversely, technical promise and hypothetical contribution cannot be promoted to a commercial GO. No new commercial permissions, payments, real customers or resource replenishment follow from this definition.

### Article calculation

The first production stage uses **20,000 Terra input tokens and 4,000 Terra output tokens**, yielding **$0.04** in published standard model rates. An entire autonomous pipeline is more involved than a single text pass. Illustrative agents also conduct sourcing, adversarial review, corrections/testing, sales proposals, customer support/delivery, and development/maintenance. Those seven phases cost about **$0.25 in assumed model tokens**. Explicit non-model costs add **$4.78**, including hypothetical tool/API use, compute, failure reserves, paid distribution, payment fees, risk and development amortization. The resulting **$5.03** is an *illustrative all-agent service cost*, **not a claim that all of this has been validated or paid**.

### Software calculation

The initial Astra patch-generation stage uses 250,000 input and 40,000 output tokens at the published $10/$50 per million standard tariffs, producing **$4.50** of model inference. Other assumed agents perform investigation, independent-style critique, corrections/testing, acquisition, delivery/support and maintenance. All seven phases total **$11.90** in assumed model API charges. Non-model compute, test infrastructure, failed attempts, acquisition/distribution, payment processing, warranty and amortized engineering add a hypothetical **$48.00**. The resulting **$59.90** includes **no mandatory human-review hour**. It does not demonstrate that the agent can deliver an accepted patch at that price.

### What gets measured rather than padded

Each case includes a named agent phase for:

- production;
- research and source checks;
- adversarial QA;
- correction and testing;
- customer acquisition;
- customer support and delivery;
- development and maintenance.

The calculation prices each agent phase at an explicit model tariff and token budget. It separately records seven non-model categories: external tools/data, compute/storage, retries/failed attempts, paid distribution, payment/delivery fees, warranty/liability reserves, and non-model development amortization.

**A $0 assumption is permitted only for a genuinely absent cost in the defined scenario.** Unknown actual costs must remain unknown in measured reporting. Expense categories should be tightened or expanded from real instrumented runs, not inflated with fictitious employees or omitted to make the numbers attractive. Higher model capability may be worth its additional inference cost if it improves acceptance, time-to-result, or reduces retries.

## Evidence and commercial experiment

First run a representative, rights-cleared job cohort **end-to-end** using the strongest suitable frontier models and qualified tools. Instrument every operation: model/version/token usage, tool calls and fees, CPU/storage, retries, QA tests, source checks, delivery, agent-run acquisition and support, refunds, failure rates, and any actual human escalation. If independent QA or customers cannot accept the autonomous result, mark the job unaccepted rather than assuming equivalent human quality. Test accepted tasks on held-out specifications and give H the same input rights and obligations.

The most useful primary outcome is **cost per independently accepted and supported job** plus completion time, not cost per generated answer. Model selection should be a measured policy: routine work may use efficient models and difficult tasks premium frontier models. A strong all-premium agent is a useful comparator if budget permits. Add a competent human counterpart, including its total human prep/review/corrections and wage burden, and record the assumptions behind expert speed.

Then run the separate **commercial pricing/adoption** experiment. Compare actual willingness to pay and customer access to alternatives. A large labor-cost saving can coexist with little producer profit if buyers can self-serve using the same AI. Do not use a historical $200 human service cost as proof of a $200 selling price; the illustrative sale prices have no demand evidence. Under existing permissions, external acquisition/sales and paid providers remain **not authorized**; the calculation does not perform them.

Finally distinguish an AI-native service that earns revenue from AGI ALPHA's deeper invention-machine hypothesis. Retain causal mechanism records with tests and failure boundaries; compare successors with inheritance versus no inheritance and equivalent ordinary reuse. Pay for the entire adaptation, checking and learning lifecycle. Reinvest **only unrestricted actually earned or explicitly labeled simulated** surplus in genuinely new construction and verify fresh benefit; customer deposits/loans are not earned resources.

## Executed commercial campaign 010

The implemented service is row-level receivables reconciliation plus an exact exception schedule on authored synthetic records. It does not attest accounts or promise resolution when source information is ambiguous. Both comparator and buyer utility must match that contract. Four illustrative reference cases remain intact and are not measured outcomes or default selling prices.

[CALIBRATION.json](campaign/commercial-010/CALIBRATION.json) records primary wage/benefit sources and task-time uncertainty. The selected human complete-service scenario uses four hours at approximately USD34.74 loaded compensation per hour, rounded to USD139 total. This treats loaded labor as a conservative floor with no invented additional overhead, assuming all comparable obligations fit the stated time. Actual task time and a matched retail quote are unmeasured; shorter human and software-assisted alternatives are sensitivity cases. Raising the input cohort to 1000 source rows per order does not itself establish four human hours.

Offer exploration starts at a 5 percent discount to that human anchor and tests higher prices. Buyer values, trust, onboarding, urgency premiums and repeat demand are conditional model parameters. Competitors may undercut our offer under their own resource costs; commodity parity and correlated adverse conditions may defeat the premium service. No AI-cost-plus selling rule or forced purchase is used.

Exact host model routing, token usage and billing remain UNKNOWN. Complete-cost setup and operating scenarios include agent development, failed search, source handling, testing, acquisition, rejected and unsold work, corrections, support, fees, tax provision and terminal obligations. These are disclosed sensitivity budgets rather than invented token receipts. A positive verdict is conditional on actual complete costs fitting the demonstrated region. No mandatory hypothetical human staff is charged to AI. Modeled taxes and provider terms are assumptions, not jurisdiction-specific financial claims.

The native ProtectedExchange remains the authority for earnings and investment gates. Seed liquidity, revenue, receivables, collection, accrued expenses, reserves and earned cash remain separate. Actual production/checking is rerun only after the next simulated funding commitment. A qualified simulated earned balance can fund simulated continuation; it cannot become a real cash balance. See the five machine-readable verdicts in the campaign result and [browser paper](docs/commercial010/REPORT.md).

The later [autonomous onboarding and strategic pricing adjustment](campaign/commercial-010/ONBOARDING_PRICING_ADJUSTMENT.md) controls price selection: maximize expected complete-cost contribution and sustainable cash growth under strategic buyer/rival responses. Choose the discount and implied win probability jointly, within the human-anchored premium offer range. A fixed five-percent policy is a controlled baseline, not a universal optimum. Separate actual agent-operated onboarding resources, uncertain customer attention/integration friction, and external obligations; one-time costs persist across repeat orders. Any optimality statement is limited to the modeled information and feasible price set.

## Current executed outcome - 30 October 2026

**Commercial 010: CONDITIONAL_SIMULATED_GO.** Actual autonomous production supplied 19,000 synthetic source rows across 19 orders: 16 deliveries passed the separate checker and three orders were rejected. The completed four-arm experiment supports fixed-capability repeated delivery financed by prior unrestricted simulated earnings; invention improvement is not a prerequisite. See the [machine-readable result](campaign/commercial-010/RESULT.json), [browser-readable paper](docs/commercial010/REPORT.md) and [compiled PDF](docs/commercial010/paper.pdf).

Across the 18 qualification-region cases, mean complete modeled profit is **$375.80**, versus **-$123.14** for fixed-discount/original-onboarding control. Cash survives in 18/18 cases; 13/18 individually qualify, while five lose or fail the repeat-funding conditions. Mean earned-funded continuation is 3.06 cycles. Selected first/repeat prices are $139/$121.625. Settled collections per scheduled slot are 73.61%, versus 76.39% for fixed pricing with autonomous onboarding. Conditional participation wins among arrivals are 81.78% versus 84.76%, and collections per win are 96.36% versus 96.49%; see [metric definitions](campaign/commercial-010/METRIC_DEFINITIONS.md). The objective is expected collected complete-cost profit and sustainable cash, not maximum win rate or the highest advertised price.

This result is conditional on the declared $500 setup/$15 cost-unit scenario, the assumed human comparator of four hours first order and 3.5 hours repeat, and unmeasured assurance/trust and customer-friction differences. Host billing and actual buyer demand remain unobserved. Original no-sale results, both interrupted executions, corrupt storage evidence, immutable source versions and the disclosed I/O-only recovery remain preserved; no favorable seed substitution or global five-verdict AND is introduced.

The separate [guarded portfolio 011 report](campaign/commercial-011/REPORT.md) screens 25 services and technically qualifies four within their declared scopes. Its guarded central projection has median $414,092.53 complete-cost profit, 107 earned-funded cycles and 11,302 settled jobs, with 10/10 cash survival over 120 months from $5,000 seed. Scaled jobs are conditional aggregate projections, not individually executed customer deliveries; finite provider-tail terms are stronger successor assumptions. **The $1 billion trailing-year target is reached in 0/90 fresh projection cases by 120 months.** Actual commerce/live revenue, capability-compounding benefit and Blue Sky enablement remain NOT_ESTABLISHED. Publication, merge and release status must follow actual source-matched gates; these results do not assert a future release is already published.
