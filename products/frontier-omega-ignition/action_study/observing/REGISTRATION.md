# Does a later public gravitational-wave notice change the review queue?

## Registered question

MONTREAL.AI’s scientific review rehearsal has one entry per public LVK candidate. An independently verifiable official retraction should remove that candidate from the shadow follow-up queue. A live candidate keeps a review entry. This issues no telescope request, and an entry represents neither a confirmed astrophysical event nor a telescope allocation.

Freeze this question, the cutoffs, comparisons and source order before retrieving cohort products. Logical sample size is finite for this experiment. It does not constrain future missions.

## Population and transfer

Use the first five distinct public, non-test S-prefixed LVK candidates in event-time order from 2024-04-10 inclusive to 2024-05-01 exclusive UTC. An official initial, preliminary or early-warning notification must exist. Use the published NASA GCN Classic LVC index when available, otherwise the public GraceDB superevent registry. Deduplicate by superevent ID. Order by trigger time then ID, never by its final disposition. If archive roster access fails, use NASA's public GCN Circulars archive for the same period, first five qualifying LVK candidate subjects in circular submission order; this fallback has different selection and coverage. Bind the route and preserve its exact returned population and exclusions. The fifth returned case is the unchanged receiver's transfer check. Do not replace a failing or unanswered case.

If the consecutive group contains no retraction, add the earliest public April event outside the group with an official retraction as a separately labelled mechanism control. If the April sources lack such a control, preserve the result. For each selected event, inspect its published status packets before the seven-day deadline after its first alert. Only an authoritative packet for the same superevent may change the recommendation. Inspect an official GCN Circular for the first retracted event when available. Record that the circular and packet report the same collaboration's conclusion.

## Evidence and action

Use official original LVK notices in NASA GCN or GraceDB, or an original circular explicitly issued by/on behalf of the collaboration when raw packets are unavailable. Distinguish trigger, packet creation, circular submission, archive retrieval and actual delivery time. Report packet decisions as a retrospective issue-time simulation. A separate live-availability claim needs a per-record public distribution or recipient-ingestion witness. The current index alone cannot establish completeness of notices historically received by a particular observer. An inaccessible/missing required packet leaves the decision unqualified until recovered. Do not infer that an astrophysical event did not occur, or that a retraction never existed, from a negative search.

Make a decision immediately before the first authorized evidence, after the first, at each subsequent status change and through the registered horizon. Preliminary/early-warning/initial/update retains an active shadow review; retraction withdraws it. Conflicting or regressing source histories require review. Preserve the immutable status packet identifier and its bytes, event and publication identities, timestamps, route and record hash. Reject mismatched event, non-observation role, changed source bytes, same-version conflicts, incomplete sequences and premature use of later data. Never reinstate a withdrawn entry solely on a non-retraction packet without a new explicit, independently qualified authority.

## Comparisons and cost

Compare the retained first notice alone, conventional latest eligible official notice in SQLite, and the integrated source-aware decision. All receive the same registered identity, cutoffs, access and source restrictions. The direct route can win. Report observed changes, agreement, unresolved cases, validations, HTTP calls/bytes/elapsed time, replay timings and unknown costs separately. Direct downloading of the official status history is already a practical alternative. A better archive action is demonstrated only if source availability changes an accepted queue entry. Actual observer time, avoided telescope visits, scientific benefit, complete operating price, buyer demand and causal advantage remain unmeasured without participating observers.

This instantiates OUT-LEARN (pp. 3–7) and OUT-STRATEGISE (pp. 3–6). The assumed link is between the authority's current issued status and a reversible queue recommendation. Event truth and human workflow efficiency are outside that link.
