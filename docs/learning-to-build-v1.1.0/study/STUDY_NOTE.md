# Complete means: a finite construction illustration

This is an executable analytical illustration. Its compiler, task language,
scenarios, rights flags and cost parameters are author supplied. It constructs and
checks actual programs, but it does not demonstrate autonomous discovery, learned
constructor improvement, physical sensing, customer acceptance or real commerce.
No model API, external payment, customer contact or native account is used.

## Reproduce

```bash
python3 study.py --output evidence
python3 check.py --evidence evidence
python3 -m unittest test_integrity.py -v
```

`study.py` writes standalone Python instruments from four exact task specifications.
`check.py` does not import the producer and separately replays generated programs,
route-stage cash flows and lifecycle arithmetic. This is a separate-source
engineering check; it is not an independent institutional evaluation.

The frozen protocol digest is
`38a4ef8101139270a903525bc75f6ce13890828eeac645e586472b7c6503e820`.
The protocol and all four tasks were written before the first program execution.
Subsequent implementation changes replaced Python assertions with explicit checks;
they changed no protocol, case or reported outcome.

This package reproduces that exact registered model; it is not a general
configurable simulator. Both entry points reject protocol bytes whose SHA-256
differs from the frozen digest, including a changed cost or receipt with a
recomputed report hash. A new model requires a separately registered study.

### Integrity corrections from independent review

The first checker accepted altered protocol parameters while fixed model constants
remained in the implementation, and it did not validate every reported field.
These were checker defects. The repaired entry points pin the original protocol,
and the separate checker reconstructs the entire result record: generated-program
counts and hashes, donor reuse, full route grid and summary, successor and control
counts, every retention row, and adverse-check flags. Exact comparison includes
types and array cardinality. Targeted mutations cover the two defects, including
false counts, false reuse, fabricated hashes, missing rows and false flags.
The original protocol and model are unchanged; regeneration changes the source
digest, not the study's outcomes. This records the correction rather than treating
the initial acceptance as adequate verification.

## What is actually constructed

An authored task specifies named raw integer fields, affine calibrations,
aggregation weights and an acceptance threshold. The compiler algebraically
combines these into a standalone executable measurement program. For example,
source task input `(a,b,c)` computes `2a + 2b + c + 1`. A fresh successor changes
the schema and computes `2q - p + 6r + 1`. Two further successors have four and five
fields. These are synthetic integer measurement specifications, not physical
calibration evidence.

The source program cannot consume the successor schemas. The retained generic
compiler constructs new executables. Every new instrument and a strong ordinary
declarative interpreter agree with independently evaluated specifications on all
inputs in the declared five-value domain `{-2,-1,0,1,2}`.

| Task | Inputs | Compiled instrument correct | Ordinary interpreter correct | Reusing source program |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---|
| Source, 3 fields | 125 | 125 | 125 | Same task |
| Successor 1, 3 fields | 125 | 125 | 125 | Schema rejection |
| Successor 2, 4 fields | 625 | 625 | 625 | Schema rejection |
| Successor 3, 5 fields | 3,125 | 3,125 | 3,125 | Schema rejection |
| Total | 4,000 | 4,000 | 4,000 | Three incompatible successors |

This establishes finite executable correctness and fresh artifact construction in
a supplied language. The conventional interpreter succeeds equally. There is no
new scientific instrument, novel compiler, discovery advantage or evidence that
the constructor itself learned. The tasks are authored fresh successor tasks,
not independently held-out natural tasks.

## Complete-means model

An internal route additionally needs a reusable testing fixture, current access
terms, fresh qualification and production resources. An ordinary external complete
service remains available under separate supplied standard terms. All costs are
paid before a modeled accepted receipt of 80 units. Costs are synthetic units,
not dollars, market quotations or measured inference/labor costs.

| Route/state | New construction | Fixture | Current terms | Qualification | Production | Total | Duration |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Fresh internal | 18 | 10 | 6 | 8 | 12 | 54 | 8 |
| Inherit fixture/artifact | 18 | 0 | 6 | 8 | 12 | 44 | 6 |
| Inherit constructor | 7 | 0 | 6 | 8 | 12 | 33 + m | 4 |
| External complete service | — | — | — | — | — | 58 (+ m if constructor retained) | 4 |

Constructor acquisition costs 9 after source collection. Maintenance `m` is a
binding current obligation in the retained-constructor state, even when using the
external service or not entering. This makes explicit why more assets need not
mean a better state. Internal current consent is a scenario flag; missing consent
or qualification forbids internal delivery. External terms do not inherit that
internal restriction. These flags do not represent consent actually obtained.

Every plan must satisfy every cash prefix. Conditional revenue cannot fund its
own prepayment. The finite grid crosses 8 cash values, 6 deadlines, 2 internal
consent settings and 3 maintenance values: **288 cells**, **864 state decisions**.
The exact finite planner and an equal-content ordinary analytical planner tie in
**all 288 constructor-state decisions**.

| Maintenance m | Cells | Accepted fresh state | Accepted artifact state | Accepted constructor state |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 0 | 96 | 12 | 22 | 40 |
| 12 | 96 | 12 | 22 | 20 |
| 26 | 96 | 12 | 22 | 0 |
| Aggregate | 288 | 36 | 66 | 60 |

These counts describe the explicitly selected grid. They are not probabilities,
market shares, generalized success rates or evidence of a learned-method advantage.
Fresh/artifact counts repeat across maintenance slices because that obligation
belongs only to the constructor state; aggregate counts should not be used as a
fair performance ranking across unequal states.

At cash 33, deadline 4, valid internal terms and zero maintenance, the complete
constructor route delivers while fresh/artifact/external routes cannot. Remove
current rights or fresh qualification and this advantage vanishes. At cash 58,
deadline 4 and no internal consent, artifact-state external service is feasible;
retaining a constructor with maintenance 12 makes it infeasible. That counterexample
violates the resource/obligation premises needed for monotone frontier expansion.

## Lifecycle accounting

Against the same fixture-only continuation, the full lifecycle saving for `n`
fresh successors is

`(54 + 44n) - [54 + 9 + n(33 + m)] = n(11 - m) - 9`.

Retention is strictly cheaper only if `n(11-m) > 9`, with all acceptance, rights,
deadline, qualification and prefix-cash conditions still satisfied. With zero
maintenance, three successors save 24 units after constructor acquisition. With
maintenance 12 or 26, no positive `n` amortizes retention under these assumptions.
An initial cash 54 can execute source construction, collect modeled 80, buy the
constructor for 9, and prepay subsequent zero-maintenance 33-unit routes. This is
arithmetic in an illustrative scenario, not a replacement for repository-native
accounting or an operating enterprise.

## Useful elementary propositions

**Qualified state dominance.** Consider a finite transition system with accepted
outcomes. If state B makes every A-feasible action sequence available, preserves
its purpose/acceptance and rights, has weakly more spendable resources at all
relevant prefixes, has no greater obligations, and has no longer action durations,
then every A-reachable accepted outcome is B-reachable. Proof: replay each
A-feasible sequence; each condition preserves feasibility step by step. This is
a conditional inclusion, not a claim that every acquisition improves the state.

**AND-complementarity defeats a generic diminishing-returns assumption.** Let
`V(S)=1` only when both instrument `i` and current qualification `q` are in S.
Then the marginal value of adding q to the empty set is zero, while its marginal
value after i is one. The submodular diminishing-returns inequality fails. This
is a counterexample to assuming greedy singleton value screens are sufficient,
not a complexity theorem or a claim that every complete-means objective behaves
this way. A strong conventional joint planner can capture the same complementarity.

**Asset enabling is distinct from construction learning.** A new artifact becoming
feasible when a supplied reusable compiler is retained demonstrates conditional
asset value. A claim that experience improved construction requires fresh tasks
and a matched control given equal content, rights and resources. An improvement
in how the constructor is acquired or changed is a further causal claim. The
present equal-content control ties, so this study supports the first distinction,
not either stronger learning claim.

## Adverse checks

The run explicitly rejects wrong schemas, a tampered task contract, missing
current qualification and absent current internal rights, and preserves a
maintenance-driven contraction of the reachable set. Every admitted route has
nonnegative prefix cash and exact receipt-minus-cost arithmetic. Actual customer
receipts are zero; actual external resource bills are not measured.
