# Why this construction

**Decision before scoring: construct a reproducible microscopy measurement instrument, then attempt a different measurement on a different assay.** This is a research construction supporting the Learning to Build programme. It does not replace the repository's GENESIS flagship or claim a new bioimage-analysis method.

## Search beyond the convenient example

We compared three materially different outcomes. The decision uses qualitative evidence and identifiable dependencies; the scores of an invented opportunity-ranking model would add little here.

## What is missing here

Microscopy software already exists. The missing means for this programme were a **connected, executable measurement construction and a controlled successor experiment**: authorized public inputs, an explicit recipe, an operator interface, independent reference interpretation, comparable construction histories, maintenance tests and a claim boundary. Adapting established methods is the selected route. No exclusive capability, market gap or algorithmic novelty is presumed.

The finite recipe bank permits complete conventional enumeration. If it is cheap and effective, we use the finding; we do not manufacture a tiny search allowance to make a learned ordering appear necessary. The acquired dataset annotations supply valuable expert work and are credited. They are not our own new measurements.

Buying a service, cooperating with a laboratory and commissioning new images remain possible future routes. They require participants, terms and authority that do not exist in this experiment. Maintaining the stronger existing method, stopping an unsuccessful method claim, and retaining cash are genuine choices. No funding, buyer, invoice, delivery agreement or outreach is created by this selection.

## Decision rule after execution

Report separately: the delivered software and measurement qualification; the same-content construction-policy comparison; successor qualification and any causal enabling effect; and economic activity. A useful instrument can survive a negative learning result. A failed successor can identify the missing means for the next test. Neither is a reason to rewrite the frozen criteria.
