A fixed-capability autonomous commercial loop under explicit simulated market conditions

AGI ALPHA Invention Flywheel Lab | Executed synthetic reconciliation, strategic buyers and audited earned-funded repetition | 30 October 2026

Abstract

We implemented and executed a bounded receivables-reconciliation service, a strategic repeated-market environment and an event-level financial auditor. The cohort supplied 19,000 authored synthetic records across 19 orders: 16 deliveries passed a separately implemented checker and 3 orders were rejected. Buyers, rival providers, free/conventional self-service and a supplier heuristic affect transactions; all customers, orders and payments are explicitly simulated. Across 168 prespecified four-arm seeded confirmation runs, the joint strategic-pricing/autonomous-onboarding arm offered $139.00 first-order and $121.62 repeat-order prices. In the declared qualification region, 13/18 runs supported repeated profitable delivery and continuation from prior unrestricted simulated earnings. This is a conditional model result over actually executed synthetic service artifacts, not observed commerce, population-level demand validation, recursive capability improvement or Blue Sky enablement.

2. Executed service production and acceptance

A bounded screen compared researched articles, tested software patches and receivables reconciliation. Reconciliation was selected for exact independently checkable outcomes. Orders contain USD integer-cent invoice and bank-payment records, customer identities, references and an explicit completeness obligation. Delivery produces a row-level JSON reconciliation and exception CSV. Referenced bundles and split payments match only at exact cents and consistent customer identity. Unreferenced payments require unique corresponding invoice and payment candidates. Overlapping exact reference groups remain quarantined.

The larger executed cohort contains 19 orders of 1,000 total source records each. Namespaced blocks synthesize diverse reference, split, bundled, unique-amount, partial, ambiguous and conflicting-reference patterns. Original completeness contracts are retained: valid exception schedules can be accepted only when the order allows unresolved findings; strict-completeness ambiguity is rejected. Unsupported CAD and a negative reversal are rejected at input validation; the strict ambiguous order receives a reconciliation that fails its completeness obligation. Thus 19,000 supplied records must not be relabeled 19,000 successfully reconciled records. The original smaller cohort and its exact source snapshots remain available; small source examples are not used to justify a four-hour human service estimate.

All inputs are authored public synthetic workloads. They are not client records or deidentified real books. Generation families and reused seed sets are disclosed; the larger execution is engineering replication, not a fresh population holdout. The unsafe-first-match output is a supplied adversarial counterexample, not a naturally observed frontier-model error rate. Its balanced arithmetic conceals an arbitrary ambiguous allocation, which the checker rejects. No tax filing, professional attestation, record modification, fraud certification or money movement is within this service contract.

The recorded production and checking CPU time for the cohort was 2.482850 seconds, with 2.483870 seconds wall time. These measurements exclude agent inference, code construction, orchestration and wider campaign work. They are not complete costs and are never converted into human work hours. Separate builder and checker roles share the execution host and model family; this is procedural separation, not external certification. Exact routed model/version, settings, token receipts and host billing were not exposed.

The added autonomous onboarding workflow creates clearly synthetic CSV exports from the frozen source records, then actually parses explicit contract metadata and invoice/payment tables into validated canonical order JSON. It supports the declared known CSV schema, exact headers, quoting/BOM, integer-cent units and explicit currency/reference checks. It does not claim an arbitrary financial-system integration or an actual customer export.

3. Strategic actors, payoffs and timing

The horizon is 8 cycles with 2 candidate deliveries per cycle, a finite cohort and bounded buyer budgets. No output can be sold twice within a run. Buyer participation is chosen from our service, a competent human-delivered alternative, an adapting rival, free/conventional self-service and no purchase. Buyers have heterogeneous latent values; arrivals, trust, switching costs, payment incentives and future reputation affect repeat choices. Rival decisions use noisy value proxies. Parameters are transparent conditional assumptions rather than observed willingness to pay. The 0.99 and 0.97 utility coefficients and trust levels are assumed ex ante reliability/value weights, not measured comparative accuracy estimates. Both completed-service routes are assessed against the same declared functional obligations; this experiment does not establish inferior human acceptance quality.

U_own = own_trust * 0.99 * value - own_price - onboarding + fast_delivery_premium
U_rival = rival_trust * rival_quality * value - rival_price - rival_switching + fast_delivery_premium
U_human = max(0, 0.97 * value - human_benchmark)
U_self = 0.99 * self_service_trust * value - self_service_cost + fast_delivery_premium
participate = arrival AND affordable AND U_own >= max(0, U_rival, U_human, U_self)

The competing provider searches a 30-point price/quality grid to maximize (price minus conditional unit cost) times estimated customer count. A reported zero residual verifies only maximization over that grid against noisy information. It is not a Nash equilibrium certificate, a continuous best response or a proof that no strategic deviation exists. The supplier uses a bounded scarcity pricing heuristic: the exogenous world term is multiplied by one plus 5% times batch utilization. Supplier terms and buyer preferences are structural assumptions; random seeds describe only variation within those assumptions.

Before each batch, the native exchange checks funding and commitments; actual deterministic service production and independent checking then run again on the selected source-bound orders. This sequencing prevents a claim that new earnings financed earlier qualification work. The actual host resource bill remains unknown: earned funds and resource payments in this demonstration are simulated. Provider investment and batch production precede buyer choice, so unsold inventory still costs resources. For a buyer who participates, consent, customer escrow funding and preparation precede release or refund; this is not order-specific consent before production. Delayed settlement, the contractual deadline, modeled revision charges, disputes, reputation updates, finite budgets and unsold work can prevent collection and repeat success. Market revision events are stochastic cost/risk draws, not separately executed corrected service artifacts; the actual artifact correction/rejection evidence is retained in the service cohort.

4. Human comparison and premium pricing

The human benchmark is $139.00 for a four-hour manual-service scenario. BLS bookkeeping wages are loaded once using a published total-compensation-to-wages ratio; the rounded loaded rate is approximately $34.75 per hour. The four hours comprise assumed 0.5 hour first-customer human onboarding/setup plus 3.5 hours recurring human preparation, reconciliation, verification, corrections, delivery and support. The repeat-order loaded-labor benchmark is therefore $121.625 rather than adding onboarding again to $139. All human-side stages remain human work at human compensation. The task times are unmeasured scenario assumptions, not performance measurements against an accountant or strong import/reconciliation tools. The policy interval is $17.38 to $278.00. It is an assumed four-hour loaded-labor comparator covering the declared scope and obligations, with zero incremental nonlabor expense. No matched completed human workflow was measured; further enterprise costs are not invented or double counted. Fast competent conventional tools can reduce human time and erode the price anchor.

The initial offer applies a 5.0% declared discount to the human benchmark. Exploration tests $125.10, $132.05, $136.22, $139.00, $145.95, $166.80, $208.50 and evaluates the joint effects of price, discount and win probability on expected collected complete-cost profit and sustainable cash. The objective is neither maximum win rate nor the highest advertised price; the feasible premium-price grid and information assumptions bound any optimum claim. The selected first/repeat prices and their price-win-profit curves are reported below; adaptive candidate policies may set different multipliers on the first and repeat human anchors. Low AI cost is retained as potential margin rather than used as a cost-plus selling-price rule. A premium list price without buyer participation cannot qualify.

The human operation performs human work at human speed with loaded compensation. Agent-side research, production, verification, correction, acquisition, support, development and maintenance are machine workloads; no default human reviewer, salesperson or developer wage is inserted. No human performed per-job preparation, checking, correction, delivery or simulated transaction execution. The owner supplied the mandate and steering clarifications; these are disclosed inputs rather than hidden service staffing. No timed human service intervention was observed. Conventional and AI-native alternatives constrain willingness to pay; their technical parity does not automatically invalidate profitable autonomous service economics versus the human comparator.

7. Robustness region, negative results and limitations

The declared qualification region comprises qualified_differentiation, moderate_differentiation, delayed_high_assurance. Its trust/assurance differentiation, buyer values and outside options are unmeasured structural assumptions. The service checker checks its narrow functional contract; it does not establish that customers grant that trust advantage or pay the modeled price. Synthetic well-specified inputs do not cover messy real exports, confidentiality operations, professional liability or unsupported accounting cases. Accepted exception-report service is not advertised as guaranteed fully resolved books.

Commodity AI parity, negligible demand and correlated adverse conditions are explicit failure controls. The correlated world combines weaker trust and arrival, stronger rival positioning, higher supplier cost, more revisions/disputes and delayed settlement. A positive result need not survive every adverse world, but failures must remain visible and may invalidate broad market claims. Conditional premiums depend on a segment that values the verified scope; no automatic near-AI-cost price cut is used to conceal failure.

The most informative next external experiment is a separately authorized blind evaluation on consented real invoice/payment exports, with actual task-time and complete-cost measurement followed by buyer choice at the premium price. That would discriminate the large unknowns: meaningful assurance differentiation, accepted exception scope, human-tool turnaround, conversion, repeat demand, corrections, collection and available cash. No outreach, new spending, credential change or real financial action is authorized by this campaign.

A separate provisional 25-service portfolio/pricing lane is linked in PR17 at source be886e674f8b9bbce76410e5810b908c06a4300d. Those hypothetical offers, win probabilities and profit illustrations are not executed service evidence from this campaign, validated buyer demand, observed revenue or proof of large-scale commercial capacity. Broader discount policies are possible future competing model controls; this frozen experiment is limited to 90-150% of the human anchor and its declared first/repeat adaptive candidate. No candidate was changed after the freeze to import a favorable portfolio result.
