# CONTINUITY Ω — First paid-pilot acceptance contract (proposed; no real approvals)

## The one falsifiable business claim

**Can a trusted, auditable business-commitment handoff make a scoped fallback fulfillment promise cheaper or more reliable than the strongest already available solution, and attractive enough that a real business accepts and pays at a price covering its complete cost?** A conventional winner or no-buy decision is a valid answer. Discovery of a missed recovery obligation is not by itself commercial success.

### Specific customer and job

- **Buyer:** operations director at a regional B2B packaging-label printer that has no second site, with owner/GM authority for commercial approval.
- **End customer:** one authorized business buyer whose order terms can be used during a bounded drill.
- **Fallback provider:** one *real* independent qualified printer offering one named label product and a specific reserved slot. Its availability and acceptance must be obtained and authenticated separately. A vague letter of intent is not a paid reservation.
- **Exercise:** an isolated, non-destructive rehearsal; no cyberattack, no live system sabotage, no production-customer data transferred without authorization.
- **Example target:** one accepted order of up to 20,000 units within 48 **agreed business hours**, only if the specific provider demonstrates that exact capacity and deadline. The 1,900-unit synthetic case is **not** evidence for 20,000 units.

### Frozen before the exercise

1. Purpose: exact customer commitment and output acceptance, error/reprint tolerances, cutoff times, deadlines, recovery point and recovery time objectives.
2. Evidence custody: original order events protected outside affected recovery systems, signature/key custody or authenticated origin, update cadence, chain of custody and uncertainty policy.
3. Permissions: specific data fields that may be shared, parties and jurisdictions, retention, restrictions and emergency activation authority. No customer document enters public repositories.
4. Provider: qualified product, real reserved time window and units, proven capacity net of other commitments, common failure domains, cancellation/refund policy, insurance, actual response channel and contingency if capacity becomes unavailable.
5. Economics: buyer cap, existing best alternatives, full acquisition/implementation/rehearsal/production/transport/verification/support/insurance/financing/legal costs, refund liabilities and protection reserves; every cash stage funded using authorized existing account machinery.
6. Comparison: (A) competent incumbent recovery and backup supplier; (B) same provider, records and resources using direct frontier AI; (C) CONTINUITY Ω challenge + handoff; (D) no purchase or customer self-provisioning. Count full lifecycle costs and all human intervention separately.
7. Tests: lost orders, stale amendments, canceled orders, duplicates, late new orders, restoration with incomplete history, supplier overbooking, correlated failure, refusal, bad output, delayed payment and failed recovery.

### Outcome adjudication

The customer and backup provider, not a self-grading AI, accept delivery. Independent verification compares produced labels (or a fully agreed non-production substitute) against artwork/specification, count, quality and deadline. Proof must preserve real data permissions. A future-capacity guarantee requires actual contract and capacity evidence.

Measure: accepted correct commitments completed by deadline; actual elapsed hours; missing/duplicated/incorrect units; permitted disclosure incidents; staff hours; complete cash flows and realized contribution; risk exposure; competitor performance. Report failures and unknowns. Claims are **per exercised order, facility and capacity window**. An approved tabletop exercise is not equivalent to completed alternate manufacturing.

### Explicit verdict ladder

| Verdict | Required evidence |
| --- | --- |
| SYNTHETIC CHECK PASSED | Published local source and checker pass the invented tests; no customer or supplier evidence. |
| AUTHORIZED LIVE DRILL | Actual permissions and a separately accepted exercise agreement exist. |
| RECOVERY HANDOFF VERIFIED | Independently adjudicated order record and fallback readiness, within stated scope. |
| CUSTOMER DELIVERY ACCEPTED | Buyer accepts the actual output under frozen terms and complete obligations are recorded. |
| PAID COMMERCIAL VALUE | Money is collected, liabilities accounted for, and the entire campaign's complete cost reconciled. |
| SUCCESSOR ENABLEMENT | Independent fresh task succeeds better/cheaper due to the retained method against an equal-content conventional comparator. |
| IMPROVED INVENTOR | Across separate fresh campaigns the discovery *process itself* improves against equally equipped strong alternatives. |

The current prototype has reached **only the first row**.

### Go / no-go

**GO for a real controlled paid rehearsal** only if actual customer and provider evidence is obtained, complete source custody and permissions are in place, the same-promise conventional comparator is specified, technical safety is reviewed, capacity/cash is truly available, and the purchase is voluntarily accepted.

**NO-GO for a production continuity or third-party certification claim** before a representative approved live exercise is executed and independently adjudicated. NO-GO for a market claim without collected payment and full cost. NO-GO for a successor claim without a distinct fresh use and causal comparison.

### Next opportunity—not yet an achieved product

If a paid drill demonstrates the handoff, test whether a network of backup producers can offer verified continuity slots for companies that cannot afford dedicated second sites. The missing means might be interoperable customer-commitment semantics, authenticated shared reservations, a capacity/priority contract, or a verification and reconciliation layer. Each must be built, negotiated and independently checked. A supplier already selling an equivalent offer at lower complete cost wins.
