The idea in one minute

Make the possibility buildable.

A valuable outcome may be out of reach because its necessary means do not yet exist together. A new instrument might need a measurement method. A working prototype might need a reliable process. A useful service might need an arrangement that people can willingly support.

Learning to build means learning to find and construct that missing combination. The result is tested against the purpose it was meant to serve. What survives—an instrument, a method, an applicable lesson, productive capacity or available surplus—can help make a different construction possible.

The deeper question is whether experience improves the ability to construct the next set of means.

The contribution, stated precisely

A complete route.
A fair test of what improved.

The proposed contribution is a connection between representation and evaluation. A construction witness links an executable policy to its dependencies, resource schedule, authority, evidence and continuing obligations. It makes the route from a changed idea to an accepted ability inspectable.

The evaluation then asks a harder question: with the same legitimate knowledge, tools and resources, does an experience-revised construction procedure build a different accepted capability better?

Systems integration, program learning and economic complementarity are established fields. This work builds on them. It contributes an operational synthesis, elementary conditional results, and executable comparisons that keep useful artifacts, successor acceptance and constructor improvement distinct.

Complete bioimage-agent evaluation also has precedents, including BIABench. This continuation makes a narrower comparison within a supplied recipe bank and separately tests a strong specialist. It makes no claim of a new general benchmark or superiority over those systems.

The complete means.

Completeness is relative to a particular purpose and its conditions. The necessary pieces must work together well enough to deliver the accepted outcome.

The completion test Can these means jointly deliver the required result, under the actual constraints, with evidence that the result was accepted?

The constructive loop

Change the game.
Build what it requires.
Learn to build better.

  1. Change the game

    Separate the valuable purpose from inherited methods. Search for a different route, representation or cooperative arrangement.

  2. Build the means

    Construct the missing pieces and their connections. Count the complete work, verify the dependencies and test the delivered result.

  3. Improve the builder

    Retain what transfers, including useful failures. Test whether it improves fresh construction against capable alternatives.

The return path matters: each verified construction can change the starting conditions of the next. Continued improvement must be demonstrated on new work.

The current frontier

What can we deliver with the means available now?

Existing tools, knowledge, capacity, rights and funding determine the currently feasible outcomes.

The construction frontier

What could become deliverable if we built the missing means?

A feasible construction path can change what is subsequently possible. Its costs, prerequisites and transition conditions belong in the comparison.

Expansion is conditional. Maintenance, obsolete methods, depleted resources or new obligations can also make the feasible set smaller.

Three achievements. Three different claims.

What, exactly, gets better?

A larger collection of tools can be valuable. Stronger claims ask whether the system can build a different tool—and whether experience makes that construction process better.

Level 01 · Retained means

Use what was built.

An existing instrument or method helps with a new accepted task.

Test: Does fresh use remain valid after qualification, adaptation and maintenance costs?

Level 02 · New construction

Build what is now needed.

Available means support the construction of a different working capability.

Test: Is there an executable new artifact and an accepted result under the new task’s conditions?

Level 03 · Better constructor

Learn to build better.

Experience improves the procedure that finds and constructs useful means.

Test: Does it outperform strong controls on fresh tasks with matched information and complete resources?

What the six-arm comparison actually tests

All six classical builders receive the same 24 recipes, target channels, calibration references and permitted environment. They can all search the complete bank. The proposed update changes candidate order using donor experience; it does not discover new segmentation operators.

The six policies used four distinct orders. A separately frozen cached-score replay tested 33 order/settings interventions (30 unique orders): all exhausted the same 24 failing candidates and selected r12. This explains convergence through the shared recipe bank and fallback rule; it is not agreement among independently prompted models or fresh image inference.

The primary contrast is a supplied greedy-coverage order versus conventional empirical ranking from the same donor table. A local advantage requires an accepted successor and fewer candidate executions without losing acceptance. Full enumeration is a complete conventional control. Why nuclei and cell bodies have different acceptance contracts

The first instrument must meet instance F1, foreground Dice and count-error gates on both the official 50-field validation and 50-field test sets. The successor must also recover internal boundaries, with F1 of at least 0.60 both across all fields and across fields containing true internal boundaries, on calibration and test separately.

High foreground overlap or an adequate count cannot replace the missing boundaries between cells. Area values are segmentation-derived pixel estimates; these gates do not establish biological area accuracy or physical units.

What the reference-assisted diagnostic tells us

Applying the unchanged nucleus artifact to the successor gives boundary F1 0.072995. Supplying manual nuclei and foreground to the selected cell recipe gives boundary F1 0.671256, Dice 0.999933 and count error 0.159935 on the twelve test fields.

The latter uses privileged reference-derived inputs and is not a qualified raw-channel instrument. A follow-up fixed-r12 diagnostic separated the branches on only the four already exposed calibration fields. Raw inputs gave boundary F1 0.250398; reference nuclei alone gave 0.195353; reference foreground alone gave 0.615828; both reference inputs gave 0.714909. Separate scoring implementations agreed on every component mask. The seed branch also contributes to foreground, so these are software-branch interventions, not isolated biological causal effects.

Foreground recovery was the better next lead. One separately frozen ordinary composition used the specialist’s actual retained mask union as foreground for the original watershed. It reached boundary F1 0.296809, below the required 0.60 and below the unchanged specialist’s calibration score. The stop rule halted it with zero test fields scored, no tuning and no new neural inference. The failure is retained; an unexecuted complete raw-input command is not claimed as a demonstrated capability.

Inspect the frozen diagnostics · Inspect the failed practical composition. All cases remain exposed, and reference-assisted passes do not establish a deployable raw-input solution.

The original BBBC007 boundary benchmark permits manual inputs and reports a different proximity measure. Its historical percentage is not directly comparable to this raw-input symmetric F1.

Measured work and the exact-version environment

The completed classical phases record 696.935 CPU seconds and 696.703 wall seconds, including donor construction and the labelled diagnostics. The completed 116-field specialist session records 4,969.507 CPU seconds and 4,970.035 wall seconds. These stage or session measurements exclude dependency setup, acquisition, independent verification, authoring, upstream pretraining, unavailable historical costs and economic costs. The classical ledger records no interrupted attempt, but the origin of 138 residual files remains unknown. Byte identity cannot distinguish copying from additional execution; any associated unrecorded work and cost remain unknown.

The reported local reproduction uses a dedicated Python 3.12.14 virtual environment with registered package versions and source hashes checked. It enables --system-site-packages, so it is not fully isolated and does not freeze every transitive binary or host component. The default isolated installer was checked separately; it did not replace the environment that produced these results. Setup receipts preserve the actual configurations.

What failed before this reproduction

The published history records a Boolean-outline decoding failure and a scalar/RGB observation mismatch. Narrow, versioned amendments repaired the interfaces before target test prediction or scoring. The shared RGB-to-L convention is generally lossy; it does not recover physical fluorescence.

The source and compact preregistration records were preserved. The original final raw-output package was not available for byte verification, so this edition uses new execution outputs. Historical costs that cannot be recovered remain unknown. The reproduction does not convert the exposed comparison into fresh hidden evidence.

How the reproduction’s files are preserved

The raw classical tree contained 416 files and failed the strict file-set check because 138 residual attempt-file copies remained alongside 278 canonical files. Every residual matched its canonical counterpart byte for byte; the cause is unknown.

The publication projection retains the 278 canonical files unchanged. All residual files and the reconstruction mapping are also retained, with no changes to reported metrics or costs. The manuscript appendix and construction guide explain the rejected raw tree and the separate checked projection.

Résumé en français · APPRENDRE À CONSTRUIRE

Comment l’intelligence apprend à créer ce qui rend de nouvelles possibilités réelles

Une possibilité utile peut rester inaccessible parce que les moyens nécessaires n’existent pas encore ensemble. Apprendre à construire, c’est apprendre à découvrir et à créer cette combinaison : connaissances, instruments, processus, ressources et arrangements entre participants volontaires.

Changer la donne → construire les moyens nécessaires → apprendre à mieux construire. Chaque construction doit répondre à sa finalité et faire l’objet d’une vérification. Les moyens conservés peuvent ensuite faciliter une autre construction. Réutiliser un outil, produire une nouvelle capacité et améliorer le constructeur constituent trois résultats distincts.

La version 1.1.0 conserve la première édition et ajoute une construction de recherche en microscopie : mesurer les noyaux, puis tenter une mesure différente des cellules entières. Le résultat utile, la qualification du successeur et l’avantage éventuel de la méthode de construction sont évalués séparément. La nouvelle exécution reproduit une comparaison déjà exposée; elle ne constitue pas une nouvelle confirmation à l’aveugle.

La méthode préentraînée satisfait les critères de recherche pour les noyaux sur les deux ensembles officiels. Son successeur pour les cellules entières échoue au critère de frontière. Les six stratégies classiques explorent toutes les recettes et aboutissent au même instrument non qualifié : aucun avantage de la méthode de construction n’est établi.

Les seuils sont des critères de recherche définis par les auteurs. Ils ne démontrent ni validation externe en laboratoire, ni découverte autonome, ni revenus réels, ni déploiement physique.

Quand nous construisons quelque chose de nouveau, nous changeons ce qu’il devient possible de construire ensuite.
