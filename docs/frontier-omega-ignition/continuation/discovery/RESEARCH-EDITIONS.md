# Selecting an instrument for MONTREAL.AI’s research collection

**Decision, November 17, 2026.** Investigate whether the lab can make its own papers easier to read and cite while retaining a direct route to the published page. A blind reader, a reader on a phone and a researcher verifying a quotation have different acceptance needs. The first instrument addresses reading order and page provenance. Screen-reader task performance merits its own observation.

The [bilingual opportunity cards](../../../../products/frontier-omega-ignition/showcase/opportunities.js) record five alternatives, dated primary sources and the decisive test for each. The search window was October 13–November 17. A release, preprint or improvement on a benchmark establishes a reason to investigate. Historical impossibility and economic viability require comparisons of complete delivery routes.

| Candidate | Strong existing route | What would justify further investment? |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Reading editions | Author HTML or tagged PDF, PDF search, conventional export and a competent editor | Readers recover the passage and its correct page more reliably or with less total effort. Include missing matches, review and correction. |
| Reusable chart data | Retrieve the original CSV or have an analyst digitize the plot | More charts recover the right series, units and values within a tolerance set before scoring. Measure cost per accepted table. |
| Reproducible experiments | Author launcher, pinned dependencies and a fresh checkout | Independent recipients regenerate the registered numerical result more often or at lower complete cost, including initial packaging. |
| Bilingual seminars | Direct transcription plus a bilingual editor | Consenting speakers’ technical terms and speaker turns survive code-switching, and readers can find the audio. Include editing and rights clearance. |
| Research claim audits | Expert review with conventional tools, or direct frontier-model review | Blinded adjudication on intact and altered claims shows more useful findings after false alarms and review time are counted. |

### Why build the reading edition first?

The lab controls both sides of a real publishing problem. The manuscript preserves semantic order. The published PDF fixes the pages people cite. The two can drift. A contemporary model can propose text, layout and order, and recent parsing research makes consequential errors easier to test. For publications with a trustworthy manuscript, using the author’s sequence removes the need to infer it again. Exact matches against PDF page text can then supply a narrowly checkable link. Repeated text and revisions leave cases for review. The model release motivates a competing route and future study; it supplies no runtime evidence for this construction. [NVIDIA’s October 21 announcement](https://huggingface.co/blog/nvidia/nemotron-retriever-world-class-agentic-retrieval) · [ParseBench-2, November 12](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.17400)

The strongest challenger raised during investigation starts one step earlier. If the author already provides an ordered edition with page anchors, link to it. If the PDF alone meets the reader’s needs, keep it. Before investing in retrofitting a back catalogue, test whether adding page anchors during publication solves the same need at lower ongoing cost. These alternatives retain access to the same source documents. AI-assisted linearization also predates the search window. [AllenAI’s olmOCR release history](https://github.com/allenai/olmocr)

The specific lesson from MONTREAL.AI’s *Out-Reflect*, pp. 3–10 and 12–17, is to find a case where the evidence can agree while the useful result is wrong. All words can be present while their order or page assignment is wrong. An evaluator should check author sequence and actual source pages separately, and look for false links on repeated passages. W3C’s two-column examples pair independently authored source files with published PDFs. Its test method adds an important observation for any broader accessibility claim: listen to the reading sequence or inspect it through the accessibility API. [Out-Reflect](https://montrealai.github.io/AGI-Alpha-Invention-Flywheel-Lab/out-reflect.html) · [W3C technique PDF3](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Techniques/pdf/PDF3.html)

### Decision after the engineering test

Recruit willing readers for a blinded crossover on rights-cleared English and French publications. Freeze the passages, reference pages, task order, permitted source materials and treatment of unanswerable tasks. Distribute the task order across readers so that learning one version does not systematically favour another. Compare the original publication workflow, conventional human-only preparation, direct frontier AI and the constructed edition. Keep a separately costed human-plus-AI arm when used. Record time to locate and cite the correct passage, mistaken citations, reader experience, production time, corrections, compute and unresolved support work. Calibrate the acceptance threshold with the actual beneficiary **before** observing results.

Use the existing route if it meets the same acceptance conditions more economically. A useful internal capability can justify retention even when an external offering has no viable margin. Dependencies for an external engagement include actual demand, agreed delivery rights, scope, acceptance and support obligations. Human time, inference billing, willingness to pay, financing, revenue and successor advantage remain unmeasured here. Nothing in the selection commits the lab to an ongoing service.
