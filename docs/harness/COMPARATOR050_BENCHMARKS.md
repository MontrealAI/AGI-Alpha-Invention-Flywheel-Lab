# Commercial benchmark evidence and unknowns

These references were checked on 1 November 2026. They provide labor-market context, not measured task duration, a delivered service quote or customer willingness to pay.

| Government reference | Observation | Scope |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [BLS Employer Costs for Employee Compensation](https://www.bls.gov/news.release/ecec.htm), released 9 September 2026 | June 2026 private-industry compensation averaged US$46.89 per hour worked: US$32.82 wages/salaries and US$14.07 benefits. | Employer compensation includes benefits; it does not cover every business overhead or establish compensation for a particular occupation/task. |
| [BLS Accountants and Auditors](https://www.bls.gov/ooh/business-and-financial/accountants-and-auditors.htm) | May 2025 median annual wage: US$83,680. | Wages, not an observed benefit-inclusive service cost. |
| [BLS Data Scientists](https://www.bls.gov/ooh/math/data-scientists.htm) | May 2025 median annual wage: US$120,230. | Wages, not a production/review-hour estimate or agency quote. |

No aggregate benefits ratio is multiplied into an occupational median and called an observed occupational compensation figure. No annual wage is converted into an assumed staffing plan. The repository's inherited task-hour and comparator-cost fields remain authored hypotheses; this report does not validate them or replace them silently. Prices in the complete-offer benchmark come from the separately declared finite price grids and outside options, not a mandatory discount to human wages or a markup near cheap AI production.

| Required complete alternative | What the experiment has | What remains unmeasured |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Competent human service | Official compensation/wage context plus explicit synthetic human outside quotes with stated scope/quality. | Actual acquisition, onboarding, production and review time; benefits/overhead for the actual provider; accepted equivalent service cost. |
| Human using AI | No measured service study. | Labor/time split, model/tools, checking, support, failure and maintenance bills. No hours are invented. |
| Autonomous frontier AI | Authored native complete-cost envelopes; actual executor is deterministic code. | Actual frontier model/inference/tool/host bills and market performance. No separately billable model call is made. |
| Customer self-service | Explicit authored scope, quality, price and delay costs in public outside options. | Empirical customer skill, effort, risk and opportunity cost. |
| No purchase | Always available to the modeled buyer; no seller receipt follows a refusal. | Empirical preference/demand calibration. |

All alternatives must ultimately be compared on the same accepted complete outcome. Compensation statistics alone cannot provide that comparison. Unknown task hours and frontier/host billing remain unknown, not zero. This evidence supports the cost-boundary questions; it does not establish that automation is cheaper, that a customer will buy, or that the commercial objective is achieved.
