## Risk and complete-cost limits

The selected central acceptance policy was at least 75% commercial completion, at least 90% cash survival, and a lower-20%-mean profit no worse than −$750. It did not require all cycles, every service or every world to succeed. Observed central completion and cash survival were 28/28; mean profit was $928.53, range $392.19–$1,366.28, and lower-20% mean $640.37. These are equal-weight finite-design observations, not calibrated real-market probabilities.

Native money conservation, authorized ownership, protection of restricted funds, actual acceptance and accounted obligations remain hard requirements in every case. A cash-preserving refusal alone cannot pass commerce.

Construction, acquisition failures, onboarding, inference, delivery, correction/support, maintenance, fees and investments are charged through disclosed resource envelopes. Actual host/model billing is unavailable and is not zero. The baseline shared setup envelope is $75. Raising it to $350 still passes both registered cases; $1,500 fails both, with mean loss $925.74. Other production, deadline and working-reserve scenarios are retained. These ranges bound the commercial claim; they are neither empirical bills nor universal business prerequisites. No CPU runtime is treated as human work time.

## Growth and controller-created constraints

The aggregate scale experiment tests costed delayed channels, finite unique demand, capacity, retention, new services and instruments. Ordinary and full adaptive share the paid action menu, observations, delays, buffers and eight-round planning budget; they differ in value-per-cost versus joint-value ranking. Fixed shares remains a restricted historical control. Most gain over fixed shares is therefore an action/policy-package effect, not evidence of special intelligence.

Under the disclosed **separate hypothetical $250,000 scale allocation**, $400 work-product economics and finite demand assumptions, all eight full-adaptive trajectories reach $1B in **trailing-year collected revenue** in months 22–23; ordinary adaptive reaches it in months 27–33. Fixed shares does not. No policy reaches $10B, $100B or $1T within the horizon. Run rate, cumulative revenue, realized trailing-year revenue and available cash are kept distinct.

Removing the prudential future-work buffer causes both full-adaptive sensitivity runs to fail; the buffer is a testable policy, not a universal law. Cost, conversion, delay and demand sensitivities preserve their separate results. These projections do not establish observed demand, finance native commitments, or show that the small enterprise has scaled. See [scale review](SCALE_REVIEW.md).
